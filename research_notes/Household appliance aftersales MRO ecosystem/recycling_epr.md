# Recycling, E-waste and EPR for Household Appliances (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: research was done via web search/fetch with limited tool budget; I could not open the CPCB/MoEFCC gazette PDFs, EUR-Lex, or the Delhi HC record directly. Many India EPR figures come from secondary sources (law-firm and compliance-vendor blogs). Items marked [UNVERIFIED-PRIMARY] need a gazette/official check.

## 1. Global E-waste Monitor 2024 key numbers, 2025-2026 updates, and appliance / temperature-exchange share

### Takeaway
The GEM 2024 (UNITAR/ITU, 20 Mar 2024, data year 2022) is still the latest edition I could find: 62 Mt generated, 22.3% documented as collected and recycled. Temperature exchange equipment (fridges, freezers, ACs, heat pumps) was ~13.3 Mt with ~27% recycling; large equipment ~15 Mt with ~34%. I found no official 2026 edition.

### Cited Findings
- 62 Mt e-waste generated in 2022, up 82% from 2010; 7.8 kg per capita; only 22.3% documented collected and recycled in an environmentally sound way — [UNITAR](https://unitar.org/about/news-stories/press/global-e-waste-monitor-2024-electronic-waste-rising-five-times-faster-documented-e-waste-recycling)
- Waste is rising ~5x faster than documented recycling; 14 Mt trashed (mostly landfilled); 5.1 Mt (8.2%) crossed borders — [UNITAR](https://unitar.org/about/news-stories/press/global-e-waste-monitor-2024-electronic-waste-rising-five-times-faster-documented-e-waste-recycling)
- Regional: Europe 42.8% documented recycling; Africa under 1%; Asia generates about half the world total (~30 Mt) — [UNITAR](https://unitar.org/about/news-stories/press/global-e-waste-monitor-2024-electronic-waste-rising-five-times-faster-documented-e-waste-recycling)
- Value: metals embedded in 2022 e-waste US$91bn (copper $19bn, gold $15bn, iron $16bn); $28bn reclaimed via urban mining; $62bn unaccounted for; reaching 60% collection and recycling would yield net benefit >US$38bn — [UNITAR](https://unitar.org/about/news-stories/press/global-e-waste-monitor-2024-electronic-waste-rising-five-times-faster-documented-e-waste-recycling)
- 2030 projection: ~82 Mt generated (increase quoted as 32% or 33% depending on place in the same page); documented recycling rate projected to FALL from 22.3% to 20% — [UNITAR](https://unitar.org/about/news-stories/press/global-e-waste-monitor-2024-electronic-waste-rising-five-times-faster-documented-e-waste-recycling)
- Heavier, bulkier categories (large equipment, temperature exchange equipment, screens) have the highest collection and recycling rates; small devices are 20.4 Mt at ~12% — [UNITAR](https://unitar.org/about/news-stories/press/global-e-waste-monitor-2024-electronic-waste-rising-five-times-faster-documented-e-waste-recycling)
- Category table (via search summary; secondary): temperature exchange equipment 13.3 Mt, 27% recycling rate, ~0.5 Mt copper in compressors/cables; large equipment (excl. PV) ~15 Mt, 34% — summarised from the GEM category table; see also [ServiceMag](https://servicemag.org/news/global-ewaste-monitor-repair) and [ITU press release](https://www.itu.int/en/mediacentre/pages/pr-2024-03-20-e-waste-recycling.aspx). Not directly verified on the primary PDF.
- Combined large + temperature-exchange ≈ 28 Mt ≈ ~46% of 62 Mt by simple arithmetic on the figures above (my calculation).
- Retired solar panels projected ~2.4 Mt by 2030 (4x 2022) — [UNITAR](https://unitar.org/about/news-stories/press/global-e-waste-monitor-2024-electronic-waste-rising-five-times-faster-documented-e-waste-recycling)
- Conflict: voxbooster.com blog pages cite a "2026 Monitor" with 68.4 Mt and 22.8% — unsourced, contradictory, no official release; treat as unreliable. Some trade coverage also quotes 68 Mt for another year/projection.

### Inferences
- Appliance categories (fridges/ACs/large equipment) are roughly half of global e-waste mass and have the best formal recycling rates, but still only about a quarter to a third documented; this is the most economically viable slice for recyclers (metal-rich, high weight).
- GEM publishes roughly every 2 years (2017, 2020, 2024); a 2026 edition may be pending but I found none.

### Gaps
- Primary GEM 2024 category table not opened; confirm 13.3 Mt / 27% and 15 Mt / 34%.
- No confirmation of any 2025-26 GEM update (check ewastemonitor.info).

## 2. India: rules, targets, EPR certificate economics, recycler base, informal sector, enforcement, refurbishing

### Takeaway
E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022 (in force 1 Apr 2023; 106 EEE categories) run EPR via CPCB portal certificates. A March 2024 amendment set a certificate price band of 30-100% of environmental compensation (~Rs 22/kg floor), which OEMs challenge in Delhi HC (interim relief Jan 2026; no final judgment found). Formal capacity (~22 lakh t/yr registered) far exceeds actual formal throughput; the informal sector still dominates.

### Cited Findings
**Rules and amendments**
- E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022, G.S.R. 801(E), notified Nov 2022, in force 1 Apr 2023, covering 106 categories; replaced "authorization" with online registration — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/); [Zerodha Chatter](https://thechatter.zerodha.com/p/how-indias-e-waste-rules-found-their); [IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/e-waste-management-rules-2022-assessing-indias-extended-producer-responsibility-framework/)
- Second Amendment Rules 2023 (G.S.R. 534(E), date not given in source); Amendment Rules 2024 (G.S.R. 164(E), March 2024) introduced the certificate exchange price band 30%-100% of environmental compensation under rule 22 — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)
- CPCB electronic trading platform guidelines Jan 2025; CPCB final Environmental Compensation guidelines 25 Aug 2025 — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)
- RoHS (rule 16): 0.1% Pb, Hg, Cr6+, PBB/PBDE; 0.01% Cd; Schedule II-C exemption for equipment placed on market on/before 1 Apr 2025; spares exempt until 1 Apr 2028 where compliant parts unavailable — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)
- Solar PV: must register and store waste, no recycling target until 2034-35 — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)
- Obligation basis: estimated waste generation = past sales deflated by average product life; new producers carry 15-20% of recent sales; used-equipment importers 100% — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)

**CPCB target schedule (CONFLICT, see flags)**
- GreenSutra: 60% for FY2023-24 and 2024-25; 70% for 2025-26 and 2026-27; 80% from 2027-28 onward — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)
- Zerodha Chatter: 60% now, 70% by FY2025-26, 80% by FY2027-28 — [Zerodha Chatter](https://thechatter.zerodha.com/p/how-indias-e-waste-rules-found-their)

## 3. EU WEEE revision, Battery Regulation, DPP; US state laws; China; Japan

### Takeaway
EU: Commission evaluation (2 Jul 2025) found only ~40% of e-waste collected and recycled; revision expected via the Circular Economy Act in H2 2026, possibly turning the Directive into a Regulation. I found no confirmation that the proposal has been published. Japan's 2001 Home Appliance Recycling Law is a mature retailer-collect/manufacturer-recycle model with consumer-paid fees. China pairs trade-in subsidies with a WEEE treatment fund.

### Cited Findings
- EU: Commission evaluation of Directive 2012/19/EU published 2 Jul 2025; call for evidence 1 Aug-6 Nov 2025; issues: scope gaps, collection shortfalls, weak CRM recovery, fragmented EPR, online seller enforcement; expected shift Directive to Regulation; ~40% collected/recycled; e-waste growing 3-4%/yr — [Valpak, 2 Sep 2025](https://www.valpak.co.uk/eu-weee-directive-revision-2026/)
- 2026 commentary: proposal expected Q3 or autumn 2026 with the Circular Economy Act; a separate review due by 31 Dec 2026; possible EUR 2/kg Own Resources charge on uncollected e-waste (unconfirmed) — [Eversheds Sutherland](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/latvia/insights/eu-weee-directive-reform-tighter-rules-rising-costs); [EEB policy brief, Aug 2026](https://eeb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Policy-brief_Rewiring-Producer-Responsibility-for-E-Waste_2026.pdf); [ERP outlook 2026](https://erp-recycling.org/news-and-events/2026/02/outlook-2026/). - China: 2025 trade-in subsidy covered 12 appliance categories (20%/15% for grade 1/2 efficiency, max 2,000 yuan); 2026 scheme (released 31 Dec 2025) six categories (fridges, washers, TVs, ACs, computers, water heaters), 15% for grade 1, cap 1,500 yuan — [Shanghai gov](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-Latest-WhatsNew/20251231/66a88a555a69487195e0d79444eee5d6.html); [Technode](https://technode.com/2025/01/09/china-launches-2025-subsidy-programs-for-electronics-home-appliances-and-vehicles/). - Japan's model (point-of-sale collection, consumer-visible fee) contrasts with India's producer-pays certificate model and suggests logistics/reverse-pickup is where retailer/service networks matter.

## 4. Recycler economics, refrigerant/hazardous handling, technology

### Takeaway
Value in fridges/ACs sits in copper, aluminium and steel, but compliant processing carries costs (refrigerant and foam blowing-agent capture, pollution control) that certificate revenue is meant to bridge. India has no mandate to recover refrigerants, so a major environmental and economic gap exists. Technology evidence is thin in my sources.

### Cited Findings
- Fridge/AC metal value (scrap-trade blog, low reliability): 3-6 kg copper in 1.5 t AC; copper Rs 550-700/kg, aluminium fins Rs 100-150/kg; fridge compressor copper windings Rs 500-1,200; separating parts yields 40-60% more than whole-unit sale — [ScrapRates](https://scraprates.in/blog/sell-old-ac-fridge-washing-machine-scrap) (prices may be stale)
- GEM: temperature exchange equipment holds ~0.5 Mt copper in compressors/cables — see section 1.
- Refrigerants: CEEW 2023 brief says India has no mandate to recover, reclaim or destroy refrigerant; recovery is practised mainly in mobile AC; not affordable for small workshops unless tools are subsidised; HCFC/HFC demand expected to fall 25-30% via India Cooling Action Plan — [IIFIIR/CEEW](https://iifiir.org/en/news/india-the-challenges-of-life-cycle-refrigerant-management); [CEEW](https://www.ceew.in/node/3159)
- Recycler cost burden (qualitative): pollution control, worker safety, traceability, hazardous component handling; Rs 22/kg floor defended as necessary for formal recyclers' investment — [Waste & Recycling Mag](https://www.wasterecyclingmag.com/top-stories/making-the-economics-of-compliant-e-waste-recycling-viable)
- Attero EBITDA margin only ~3% on Rs 961 cr FY25 revenue — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/entrepreneurship/news/e-waste-recycler-attero-scale-soars-22x-to-rs-961-cr-in-fy25-pat-remains-flat-9e54d5e0-1ebd-442a-a485-b1ff7db59b7e)
- Japan law mandates CFC/HCFC/HFC recovery from ACs and fridges — [METI](https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/recycle/main/english/law/home.html)

### Inferences
- Thin recycler margins plus no refrigerant mandate means formal recyclers compete on certificates; if floor price is struck down, margin pressure on fridge/AC processing could rise.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on robotic dismantling, AI sorting, recovered-HFC value per kg, compressor-line capex, hazardous waste (foam, mercury, PCB capacitors) disposal costs, or per-unit fridge/AC recycler P&L. Not searched adequately.

## 5. Reuse vs recycling hierarchy; refurbishment and EPR interaction

### Takeaway
India's rules give refurbishers a formal route: register on CPCB portal, issue refurbishing certificates that defer producer EPR by the extended life. GEM-side evidence says repair/reuse is the front line for the global waste problem. I found limited detail on how certificate quantities are computed.

### Cited Findings
- Refurbishing certificates defer EPR obligation by extended product life; 72 refurbishers (92,042 t/yr) registered Feb 2025; ~75-80 active per Toxics Link — [GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/); [IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/e-waste-management-rules-2022-assessing-indias-extended-producer-responsibility-framework/); [Toxics Link](https://toxicslink.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Stakeholder%20Consultation%20on%20EPR.pdf)
