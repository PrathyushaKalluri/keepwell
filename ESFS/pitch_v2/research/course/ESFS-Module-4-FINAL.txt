Early Stage Startups Are Generally
Valued on Revenue Multiples
 Early Stage Startups need a different metric for valuation
    Investment in growth will take away all profits
    Hence, low or no profits
    Typically, growth is faster than that of established companies
 Revenue multiple is the most widely used metric (3-10X of revenues)


   Company Value = Revenue * Multiple
Revenue Multiples: How They Work?
 Simple formula: Valuation = Annual Revenue × Multiple
 Multiple reflects investor confidence in growth trajectory
 Different multiples for different stages and investor types
 Realistic early-stage multiples: 2-10X of revenues



     Valuation = Annual Revenue × Revenue Multiple

Method 2: Scorecard Method
What it is: Get a Comparable or Agreed Multiple

Then, adjust the multiple in the reference valuation based on qualitative criteria.

Market used: Early-stage angel/VC

Key steps
1. Take a recent valuation of a similar startup as a baseline reference.
2. Adjust based on team, market, product, traction quality.
3. Use weighted scores and multiplier adjustments to calculate the final value.

1x Non-Participating Preference

Guarantees the investor gets back either

   their original investment (the “1x”) OR
   their pro-rata share of sale proceeds—whichever is greater.
Investor Payout Options

  Investment amount + unpaid dividends                                        25.00% pro-rata share of proceeds

This clause ensures the investor is protected if the company sells for a low amount, but also allows them to benefit from a successful exit.
No Double-Dip
A key aspect of "non-participating" preference is that there is no "double-dip." The
investor chooses between their preference amount or their pro-rata share, but cannot
have both. This is generally more favorable for founders than a participating
preference.

Tag Along and Drag Along Rights
Feature             Tag-Along Rights                                         Drag-Along Rights

Who Initiates       Minority shareholders (e.g., founders, employees)        Majority shareholders (e.g., investors, founders)

Purpose             Protect minority from being left out in a sale           Force minority to join a sale initiated by majority

When Used           Majority sells to third party, minority wants to join    Majority wants to sell, minority refuses to sell

Mechanics           Minority can “tag along” and sell shares at same terms   Majority can “drag along” minority to sell at same terms

Example             Founders sell 52.5%, ABC Ventures (25%) can tag along    ABC Ventures + Founders (77.5%) can drag angels/ESOP

Protection For      Minority shareholders                                    Majority shareholders

Typical Threshold   No minimum % required (any minority can tag along)       Usually 70%+ majority required to drag along

Key Benefit         Ensures minority gets exit opportunity                   Ensures buyer gets 100% ownership

Key Limitation      Minority must act quickly (usually 5 business days)      Majority must act in good faith, not to disadvantage
