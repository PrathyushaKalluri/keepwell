# Keepwell: competitors, analogs, exits and funding benchmarks
Prepared 19 Nov 2026 by research agent C and saved by the lead. It builds on `pitch/archive/market_intel/raw/competitors/` and `pitch/RESEARCH_INSIGHTS.md`. Detail and sources are in `analogs_exits.csv`, `funding_benchmarks.csv`, `competitor_retail_service.csv` and `sources.csv` (97 sources).

**Labels:**
- **[V]** filing, regulator or reputable press
- **[C]** company claim
- **[S]** secondary or aggregator source
- **[I]** inference or our own arithmetic

UNKNOWN means we searched and found nothing. It does not mean zero.

**Thin areas.** The web-search budget ran out, so three areas are thin: installation-only players, brand-to-dealer installation schemes, and failed attempts at retailer-led service.

## I7 / S3: Who already does retailer-linked installation and service?
Each piece of the job already has a provider:
- **Installation paid by brands:** 247around, SERVIZ and Qdigi.
- **Installation for e-commerce:** Jeeves (Flipkart), and Amazon through brands and partners.
- **Retailers' own service arms:** resQ (Reliance) and ZipCare (Croma).
- **Protection plans sold at the counter:** Onsitego, Zopper and OneAssist.

**No one visibly positions itself as the per-job service arm of independent Tier 2/3 retailers** (installation, then repair, then AMC). The closest attempts all stopped treating the retailer as the payer:
- Onsitego sells plans to retailers.
- 247around and SERVIZ sell to brands.

Confidence: medium. Informal tie-ups between shops and technicians don't show up in web research.

- **247around (Servify group).**
  - **Model:** multi-brand installation, in-warranty and out-of-warranty repair, and buyback. Brands pay, and the work is done through partner service centres. Logos include Amazon, Haier, Sharp, Hisense, Acer, BenQ, OnePlus, Bose, boAt, Harman and Zebronics. It claims "17,00,000+ customers" and "150+ brands, ~19,000+ PIN codes" [C]. The PIN-code figure is marketing, since 19,000 is roughly every PIN code in India.
  - **Origin:** it pivoted from LocalOye in 2015 [S].
  - **Acquisition:** Servify bought it in Feb 2022 at an undisclosed price [V].
  - **Financials:** about ₹11.4 Cr revenue in FY24 and about ₹14.5 Cr in FY25, with a net loss of about ₹3.2 Cr [S] (Inc42). That is about ₹1.2 Cr a month after ten years [I].
  - **What brands pay:** UNKNOWN.
- **SERVIZ (Noida).**
  - **Pricing proxy:** the brand pays ₹1,000 per install, the service centre gets ₹700 and SERVIZ keeps ₹300 (30%) [S] (Startup Pedia, Feb 2025).
  - **Reach and clients:** it claims 16,000+ PIN codes and 250+ cities. Clients include about 30 brands, Amazon, Zopper and Onsitego.
  - **Funding:** bootstrapped on ₹60–70 L, then a seed round in 2024.
  - **Product:** its software includes a retailer panel, but it sells to brands.
  - **S3 (why not subcontract to a 247around or SERVIZ?) [I]:** a subcontractor gets about 70% of the job and owns neither the retailer nor the household. Take in-warranty brand installs only as filler work for technicians.
- **Onsitego and Qdigi: the closest threat.**
  - **The pivot:** Onsitego started in pickup-and-drop repair, but "the cost of going after customers and returns were not in sync" [V] (YourStory 2020). It moved to retailer partnerships: Amazon, Croma, Vijay Sales, Viveks, Value Plus and Great Eastern.
  - **What it sells:** extended warranties, damage protection, AMC and repair. It does no installation [C].
  - **Network:** 400–450 brand-authorised centres and about 6M customers (2021) [C].
  - **Qdigi:** Onsitego bought it for ₹80 Cr cash in Feb 2024. Quess had bought it for ₹30 Cr in 2018 [V]. Qdigi does repair only.
  - **Financials:** FY24 revenue about ₹239 Cr with a loss of about ₹42 Cr; FY25 about ₹278 Cr [S]. "₹900–1,100 Cr" is GMV; **never cite it as revenue**.
- **Reach in smaller towns.**
  - **Jeeves:** claims 300+ centres, 1,000+ partners, about 9,000 engineers and 400 cities [V]. Next-day delivery plus installation is offered only in the top 13 cities [V], so fast installation is a top-city promise.
  - **Amazon:** installation "across 200 cities" [C]. It uses 247around and SERVIZ, and brand installation is available only in some PIN codes.
  - **resQ:** has a brand-wise installation rate card (window AC ₹590 for Voltas, Lloyd and LG; ₹531 for Kelvinator and BPL) [C]. It serves Reliance customers only.
  - **Zopper:** plans and repair, no installation.
  - **Servify:** about 51% of its revenue is from the US.
  - **Urban Company:** 51 cities (30 Jun 2025) [S]. Its top 10 cities are over 90% of the value of jobs on the platform. A "Tier 2 is the second growth engine, about 37% growth" remark is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **AC installation prices.**
  - Voltas: ₹1,199 + GST for a split AC and ₹499 + GST for a window AC [S].
  - Lloyd: about ₹1,500 for a split AC and ₹500 for a window AC [S].
  - Urban Company: window AC installation from ₹799.
  - LG: brackets and pipe are charged extra [C].
  - So **a ₹650 blended installation fee only holds for categories other than AC.** ₹1,200–1,800 for an AC is consistent [I].
  - **What brands pay dealers per install: UNKNOWN.** This is the first interview question.
- **Has anyone tried a retailer service arm and failed?** No clean failure exists. What exists is a pattern of moving away from running the jobs:
  - Onsitego moved from consumer repair to plans sold through retailers (2020).
  - LocalOye pivoted into 247around, which earns about ₹14.5 Cr a year at a loss.
  - SERVIZ moved from operations to brand-paid jobs plus software.
  - Bigfix moved to B2B (Warrantify).
  - Qdigi changed hands at ₹30 Cr, then ₹80 Cr.
  - Housejoy, Taskbob, Doormint and Zimmber failed or were absorbed.

  **Reading [I]:** the retailer-led model in Tier 2/3 is **untested, not disproven**. Confidence: low to medium.

**Multiples by model**

- **Marketplaces:** median 2.3x net revenue (2025), against a long-run average of 5.6x [S].
- **Software for one industry (vertical SaaS):** 7.4–7.8x [S].

**I15: gross vs net revenue (Ind AS 115 / IFRS 15, principal vs agent).**
- A business that controls the service and sets the price books the full job value as revenue (gross). A business that only arranges it books its fee (net).
- Urban Company uses a mix of both. It keeps about 36% of the value of jobs on its platform (Q1 FY27: ₹1,465 Cr of job value, ₹528 Cr of revenue) [V].
- Keepwell sets prices, guarantees the work and carries the rework, so it is the **principal**. It should report gross revenue, and show the job value, contribution and its take alongside. Don't present it as a marketplace. Valuation would be on operator multiples of 2–3x.

## I13 / I14: Indian funding benchmarks
- **Typical round size.** The median seed round is $1M (about ₹8.5 Cr) [V] (Inc42, Q3 2025 and H1 2026). In 2025 there were 409 seed deals worth $994M [V] (Entrackr).
- **Funds with fixed terms:**
  - 100X.VC: ₹1.25 Cr for 15% on an iSAFE, about ₹8.3 Cr post-money [C].
  - Antler India: up to ₹4 Cr.
- **Angel networks.** IAN invests $30K–600K. IPV invests $100K–1.5M. LVX did 50 deals in 2025. No published median valuation for angel rounds exists.
- **Instruments.**
  - **iSAFE** is legally a CCPS.
  - **Convertible notes** need at least ₹25 L per investor in one tranche, plus DPIIT recognition.
  - **Angel tax** was abolished from AY 2025-26.
  - The course's MediQuick example is ₹2.5 Cr in CCPS at ₹7.5 Cr pre-money.
- **Keepwell range [I]:** **₹1.5–2.5 Cr at ₹6–12 Cr pre-money** on an iSAFE or CCPS. ₹2 Cr at ₹10 Cr needs pilot data or retailer letters of intent. Without them, expect ₹6–8 Cr pre-money.
- **Series A.** The bar has roughly doubled to about ₹16–20 Cr+ revenue run-rate. Investors look for gross margin above 40% for services, a path to positive contribution in each city, cohort retention, and a burn multiple below 2.5x. On the pitch's numbers that means about 320–400 retailers, or 11–13 clusters. **Expect a ₹8–15 Cr seed round in between.**
- **Funded Indian companies in adjacent areas:** Snabbit ($5.5M A at about $23M, rising to a $56M D at about $350M), Pronto ($2M seed at $12.5M, $11M A, $45M B), Recykal, Karo Sambhav (₹56 Cr in 2026) and Namo eWaste (SME IPO). **Investors are funding house help and e-waste, not appliance repair.**
- **Government money:**
  - **SISFS** reportedly closed to new applications on 31 May 2026 (verify on the official portal).
  - **Fund of Funds 2.0** (₹10,000 Cr) invests only through funds.
  - **CGSS** guarantees up to ₹20 Cr.
  - **Telangana:** a ₹1,000 Cr state fund of funds (announced Dec 2025, no terms yet) and T-Fund (₹25 L–1 Cr).
  - **AP Startup Policy 4.0:** grants up to ₹15–20 L, seed equity up to ₹50 L and go-to-market support up to ₹50 L.

## I8: What makes analog businesses defensible
- **Asurion:** contracts with phone carriers, plus repair capacity it owns (uBreakiFix).
- **HomeServe:** affinity contracts with 1,300+ utilities that share revenue, plus books of customers it bought.
- **Urban Company:** trained supply (₹250 Cr+ spent on training), density in each neighbourhood, and 82% of job value coming from repeat users.

**Pattern:** the moat is (1) exclusive multi-year contracts with whoever owns the customer at the point of purchase, plus (2) dense, controlled, trained supply. Data and AI make both cheaper but are not the moat.

## What it means for positioning and the round
1. **Pitch "HomeServe for independent retailers", not "Urban Company for small towns".** Use exclusive retailer agreements with a revenue share. Present orchestration as the engine that keeps costs low.
2. **Name the incumbents.** Onsitego is the closest. 247around and SERVIZ show that brand-paid installation stays small. Urban Company is moving into Tier 2 (verify).
3. Take brand-paid installs only as filler work.
4. Report gross revenue, with job value, contribution and take shown alongside, and benchmark against operators at 2–3x.
5. **The round:** ₹1.5–2.5 Cr at ₹6–12 Cr pre-money. Plan an ₹8–15 Cr seed before a Series A at about ₹16–20 Cr run-rate. Don't count on SISFS; use AP Policy 4.0 and Telangana (T-Hub, T-Fund).
6. **Validate:**
   - Do brands pay dealers for installs?
   - Would Onsitego or Zopper be a partner or a blocker?
   - Which Tier 2 cities is Urban Company in?
