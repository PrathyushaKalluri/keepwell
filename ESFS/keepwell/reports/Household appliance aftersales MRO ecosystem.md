# Regulation and consolidation are reshaping appliance after-sales

As of November 2026, household-appliance after-sales is not one industry but five loosely coupled ones: OEM and third-party repair networks, warranty and protection underwriting, spare-parts distribution, refurbished and rental resale, and regulated recycling. Money is concentrated in protection, not repair. Asurion agreed to buy Domestic & General (D&G) for a reported **GBP 2.1bn** (a Bloomberg leak, not an official figure), Assurant's Global Lifestyle segment is growing at a mid-to-high-teens rate, and Frontdoor earns roughly a **27% adjusted EBITDA margin** guided for FY2026. Repair itself is a fragmented, low-growth service (syndicated forecasts cluster at 3-4.5% CAGR). It is being pushed by law: the EU Right to Repair Directive 2024/1799 applied from **31 July 2026**, but transposition is patchy. US coverage is state-by-state, with Connecticut the clearest appliance law and no federal statute. India has no binding right to repair, yet its e-waste EPR regime is the most commercially consequential rule for appliance makers and is mired in a Delhi High Court fight over the certificate price floor. India's consumer-facing winners are Urban Company (listed, loss-making after its quick-service push) and OEM networks like Samsung's; the technician base is mostly informal. AI and IoT are real but mostly vendor-evidenced, and OEMs use connectivity to pull service into their own networks rather than open it. This report labels every conflicting, single-source or unverified claim, and closes or narrows the verification gaps from the earlier overview (section 12 is answered in the final section).

## Protection is where the profit pool sits, and it is consolidating

The clearest financial signal in the landscape is the scale gap between protection underwriters and repair marketplaces. Asurion announced the D&G acquisition on 2 Dec 2025 (sellers CVC and ADIA; terms undisclosed in the release) ([Insurance Times](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/news/domestic-and-general-acquired-by-us-based-insurance-firm/1457181.article)), and Bloomberg put the price at GBP 2.1bn ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2025/12/03/849663.htm)). A summary of Asurion's release says it completed on **8 Oct 2026** ([Asurion](https://www.asurion.com/press-releases/asurion-completes-acquisition-of-domestic-and-general/)); the release itself was not fetched, so completion is single-source. Moody's rated a new USD 2.4bn term loan Ba3 on 11 Feb 2026 ([ResearchPool](https://app.researchpool.com/provider/moodys-investors-service/moodys-ratings-rates-asurions-first-lien-term-loan-ba3-iPKS0NpPw7)). Asurion's revenue is unresolved: about USD 8.5bn (S&P, LTM Sept 2022) against USD 4.6bn (2021) and USD 5bn (Apr 2025) on aggregators; its "230M+ customers" is its own claim, a third-party profile says 300M (unverified), and a "70% of handset protection" share is single-source and unverified ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/asurion-revenue)). No CMA or FTC review of D&G was found.

The listed players show where growth is. Assurant's Q3 2026 Global Lifestyle revenue was USD 2,661.1M versus USD 2,406.2M a year earlier (about +10.6%, computed), with segment adjusted EBITDA of USD 236.9M (+15%) and the FY2026 outlook raised a third time ([Assurant 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001267238/000126723826000046/aiz-20260930.htm)). FY2025 Global Lifestyle adjusted EBITDA was USD 801.3M ([Assurant release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260210146531/en/Assurant-Reports-Strong-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results)). Allstate Protection Plans (SquareTrade) reported Q3 2026 revenue of USD 631M (+7.3%) and profit of USD 47M (+38.2%); that comes from one aggregator and the implied arithmetic against Q3 2025's USD 588M is consistent, but it is not checked against Allstate's release ([finsee.ai](https://finsee.ai/earnings/all/2026/q3/en/)). Frontdoor (American Home Shield) posted Q3 2026 revenue of **USD 655M (+6%, half price, half volume)**, adjusted EBITDA of USD 205M, a new USD 1bn buyback and FY2026 revenue guidance of USD 2.205-2.215bn ([Frontdoor release](https://www.webull.com/news/15871082116127744)); a Panabee article shows USD 645M/220M, which looks like Q2 and is rejected. My computed margins show the structure: Frontdoor about 27% (direct to homeowner), Assurant Global Lifestyle about 8.9% and Allstate Protection Plans about 7.4% (channel-based, with retailer and carrier commissions). That margin reading is inference; **no loss ratio, attach rate or commission data was found for any player**, and the "60%+ gross margin" and "34% Apple attach" figures from a blog are unverified.

Enforcement is local and episodic. Choice Home Warranty settled an Arizona AG suit for USD 11.8M (judgment 23 Jan 2026, no admission) ([Arizona AG](https://www.azag.gov/index%2Ephp/press-release/attorney-general-mayes-announces-118-million-settlement-choice-home-warranty)); the FTC is renewing its Pre-Sale Availability Rule for warranty terms (notices 30 Mar and 22 Jun 2026) ([PolicyRisk](https://policyrisk.com/research/federal-register/2026-06056)), and its service-contract actions have targeted auto products (CarShield about USD 10M) ([CBT News](https://www.cbtnews.com/ftc-slams-carshield-10m-scam-exposed/)). Home warranties remain a state patchwork. Market-size figures here are order-of-magnitude only: global extended warranty is about USD 147-156bn for 2025 across IMARC, Market.us and GII, but all-category definitions differ, and the consumer-electronics segment disagrees by USD 12bn between two reports (USD 68.2bn vs USD 79.92bn) ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/global-extended-warranty-market); [Market.us](https://market.us/report/extended-warranty-market/)). Not found: any sourced revenue for AIG/Amtrust or Allianz Partners, and Choice Home Warranty's scale.

## Law is moving toward fair-terms repair, but unevenly

**EU.** Directive 2024/1799 was adopted 13 June 2024 and its national rules apply from 31 July 2026. It requires manufacturers of Annex II products to repair at a reasonable price, bans unjustified hardware or software barriers, requires publication of repair information and indicative prices, and adds **12 months to the legal guarantee** when the consumer picks repair over replacement ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/directive-repair-goods_en); [European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20240419IPR20590/right-to-repair-making-repair-easier-and-more-appealing-to-consumers)). Delegated Directive (EU) 2026/74 of 12 January 2026 added space heaters to Annex II. Transposition is patchy. Germany's Reparaturgesetz is in force from 31 July 2026 (passed 25 or 26 June, a one-day source conflict) ([Cleolabs](https://www.cleolabs.co/en/blog/eu-right-to-repair-transposition-2026); [myDPP](https://mydpp.app/en/knowledge/right-to-repair-national-transposition-status)); one other roundup says in force 23 July, which conflicts and needs national verification ([Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/26277/right-to-repair-europe-country-by-country)). As of 8 Sep 2026 Poland (bill UC154, end-2026 at earliest), Spain (likely 2027), Italy and the Netherlands had not finished. Italy later appears to have completed in October (7 Oct per one summary; decree 176/2026 in force 22 Oct per another; both single-source). **Unverified:** that the Commission sent formal notices to 18 states on 25 Sep 2026, naming France, Spain, the Netherlands, Poland and Portugal. It appears in two secondary roundups but was not confirmed against a Commission press release, and myDPP itself does not confirm the count. Also unverified: a 31 July 2029 reporting date for national repair-promotion measures (single secondary source).

The earlier "2026 vs 2028" platform conflict resolves into three dates. Member States notify national contact points by 31 July 2026; the Commission must build the common online interface by 31 July 2027 under Article 7 (per an EC-hosted summary, EUR-Lex text not read); and the Commission's page expects it operational in January 2028 ([Circular Cities and Regions](https://circular-cities-and-regions.ec.europa.eu/support-materials/eu-regulations-legislation/directive-eu-20241799-repair-goods)). No source supports a 2026 launch. Spare-parts obligations already exist under ecodesign: washing machines (Reg. 2019/2023) need at least 10 years of part availability with 15-working-day delivery ([Commission Q&A](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/qanda_19_5889/QANDA_19_5889_EN.pdf)); the package is 7-10 years across product types, with restricted access for professional repairers that BEUC criticised ([BEUC](https://www.beuc.eu/publications/eu-makes-5-household-appliances-safer-and-repairable-does-not-go-full-mile)). Regulation numbers other than 2019/2023 were not verified here. Digital product passport (DPP) infrastructure is live (registry operational 20 July 2026 per the Commission) ([Commission DPP page](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport_en)), but no appliance delegated act exists, each act carries at least 18 months' transition, and a vendor blog listing dishwashers and washers as 2026 candidates is weak and contested. My inference is that an appliance DPP is unlikely to bind before about 2028-29. The battery passport (Regulation 2023/1542) is mandatory from 18 Feb 2027 for EV, light-means-of-transport and industrial batteries above 2 kWh ([Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/consumer-goods-retail_1/european-union-sustainable-batteries-regulation-published-in-official-journal)), which is vendor-sourced and not an appliance rule. National schemes diverge: France pays a QualiRépar bonus of about 50 EUR on large appliances (one source says a 60 EUR cap; contested) and replaced its repairability index with a durability index for washing machines from 8 April 2025 ([info.gouv.fr](https://www.info.gouv.fr/actualite/avant-dacheter-consultez-lindice-de-durabilite-des-produits)); Austria's Reparaturbonus exhausted more than 130M EUR and ended 26 May 2025, then was replaced by a narrower Geräte-Retter-Prämie (cap 130 EUR, about 30M EUR a year planned), whose status is **contested** between a June 2026 end-due-to-cuts headline and an October "last chance" piece ([The International](https://www.theinternational.at/austria-replaces-repair-bonus-with-new-device-saver-program/); [Salzburg24](https://www.salzburg24.at/news/oesterreich/letzte-chance-auf-reparaturbonus-frist-fuer-geraete-retter-praemie-endet-bald-art-343040)).

**US.** There is **no federal right-to-repair law**. Faegre Drinker's 30 July 2026 survey counts about 13 states with some statute, but most cover electronics ([Faegre Drinker](https://faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/unlocking-the-right-to-repair-the-expanding-compliance-landscape)). The count is definitional and contested (PIRG called Washington the 8th state; H2 Compliance lists Texas as 8th). Connecticut is the clearest appliance case, covering "electronic or appliance products" from 1 July 2026 with AG-only enforcement; its 3-year and 5-year resource-duration tiers are unverified (statute not read) ([CBIA](https://www.cbia.com/news/manufacturing/consumer-protection-bill)). New York exempts home appliances with an embedded digital product. Washington's law (effective 1 Jan 2026) and Oregon's and Colorado's ban parts pairing, but whether Washington covers appliances is contested between ServiceMag and Faegre. The federal Fair Repair Act (H.R. 7404) was introduced 5 Feb 2026 and sits in committee ([PolicyRisk](https://policyrisk.com/research/legislation/HR7404)). FTC v. Deere settled 8 July 2026, but that concerns farm equipment. The best appliance evidence is dated: a Feb 2023 PIRG/iFixit survey found about 9 in 10 manufacturers refuse to share repair manuals even with certified professionals, with LG and Samsung the worst ([iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com/News/71693/appliance-repair-pros-struggle-to-find-lg-and-samsung-documentation)). Not verified: California SB 244's appliance scope, Colorado and Oregon appliance coverage, and any FTC action against an appliance OEM.

**India.** The Department of Consumer Affairs launched a voluntary Right to Repair Portal in December 2022 (some sources say 2024; conflict) and a committee under Bharat Khera developed a Repairability Index whose first phase covers smartphones and tablets with OEM self-declaration ([India.gov.in](https://www.india.gov.in/right-repair-portal-india); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/govt-to-introduce-a-repairability-index-for-smartphones-and-tablets-474723-2025-05-05)). Consumer durables were named an initial sector, but **nothing binding for appliances was found** and no 2026 DoCA notification surfaced. Right to repair is not a statutory right in India ([Legal Service India](https://www.legalserviceindia.com/Legal-Articles/?p=34227)).

## India: a fragmented market anchored by OEM networks, one listed marketplace and EPR

Urban Company is the dominant listed home-services platform, though its appliance exposure is undisclosed. Q4 FY26 revenue growth conflicts across sources (71% vs about 43%); the FY25 profit probably includes a one-off, which was not verified. Its own-brand Native RO purifier line had FY26 revenue of Rs 267 cr (+130%) and a reported 75% filter renewal rate in an early cohort, which is management-sourced; my inference is that Native converts the service base into a hardware-plus-filter annuity and hedges disintermediation ([UC IR](https://investorrelations.urbancompany.com/announcements-and-highlights/urban-company-launches-native-m3-pro-with-3-year-unconditional-warranty-and-long-life-filtration)). OneAssist FY25 operating revenue was about Rs 623 cr (+22%) with EBITDA of about Rs 26 cr and a net loss of about Rs 11 cr, provisional; Inc42 lists a lower Rs 517.6 cr+ (conflict) ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/oneassist-posts-over-rs-620-cr-revenue-in-fy25-with-rs-26-cr-ebitda-10067658)). A "1 in 2 households cannot afford to repair white goods" claim is single-source ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/why-india-needs-right-to-repair-1-in-2-households-unable-to-repair-whitegoods-and-appliances-due-to-prohibitive-cost/67798.html)).

## E-waste EPR turns appliance service networks into reverse-logistics players

Appliances are the best-recycled slice of a poorly recycled stream. The Global E-waste Monitor 2024 (data year 2022) counts **62 Mt generated, only 22.3% documented as collected and recycled**, with projections of about 82 Mt by 2030 and a falling recycling rate of about 20% ([UNITAR](https://unitar.org/about/news-stories/press/global-e-waste-monitor-2024-electronic-waste-rising-five-times-faster-documented-e-waste-recycling)). Temperature-exchange equipment (fridges and ACs) is reported at about 13.3 Mt with about 27% recycling, and large equipment at about 15 Mt and 34%; these figures come via search summaries and were not checked on the primary PDF. A "2026 Monitor" with 68.4 Mt on voxbooster.com is unsourced and treated as unreliable, and no official 2025-26 edition was found. A recycler-economics note is also needed: Scrap-blog copper estimates (3-6 kg in a 1.5 t AC; part separation yields 40-60% more than whole-unit sale) come from a low-reliability scrap blog with stale prices ([ScrapRates](https://scraprates.in/blog/sell-old-ac-fridge-washing-machine-scrap)).

India's E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022 (in force 1 April 2023, 106 EEE categories) run on CPCB-portal EPR certificates denominated in kg, valid two years ([GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/); [Zerodha Chatter](https://thechatter.zerodha.com/p/how-indias-e-waste-rules-found-their)). Recycling targets conflict across four sources: 60/60/70/70/80% for FY2023-24 through 2027-28 onward (GreenSutra, Zerodha, IEA) versus a superseded draft table of 60/70/80 (Enviliance); the best-supported schedule is 60/60/70/70/80, but Schedule III of the gazette was not opened, so it is **unverified against the primary text**. A March 2024 amendment (G.S.R. 164(E)) set a certificate price band of 30-100% of environmental compensation, giving the widely cited **Rs 22/kg floor**; one source says a Sept 2024 rate card also lists Rs 34/kg for a second category (paywalled, unverified), and the sequencing against the final CPCB compensation guidelines of 25 Aug 2025 is unclear. Market prices were reportedly Rs 10-50/kg in 2024-25 and below the floor, per Zerodha and Newslaundry (unverified against the CPCB portal). In April 2025 Daikin, Samsung, LG, Havells, Voltas and others challenged the floor in the Delhi High Court, which in January 2026 stayed the regulator's price declaration for Blue Star ([Newslaundry](https://www.newslaundry.com/amp/story/2026%2F01%2F14%2Fblue-star-gets-temporary-relief-as-delhi-hc-stays-regulators-e-waste-price-declaration)); sources through September 2026 report no final judgment (the Delhi HC record itself was not opened). Industry claims the floor raises compliance cost up to 4x, and Bajaj Electricals' EPR bill was about Rs 9.5 cr, rising to about Rs 18 cr (Q4 FY25).

Volumes conflict. CPCB-basis generation was about 13.98 lakh tonnes in FY2024-25 (+11.45%), whereas Down To Earth (4 May 2026) cites 6.2 million tonnes for FY24, about 10% formally recycled, and formal capacity of about 2 Mt, roughly 4x the CPCB figure and likely a methodology difference ([Waste & Recycling Mag](https://www.wasterecyclingmag.com/top-stories/making-the-economics-of-compliant-e-waste-recycling-viable); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/waste/rs-51000-crore-worth-of-materials-in-indias-e-wastemost-slips-through-the-system)). Formal share ranges 10-25% (DTE about 10%, IMPRI about 20%, Zerodha 25%). Registered capacity was 322 recyclers and 22,08,918 t/yr as of 9 Feb 2025, plus 72 refurbishers at 92,042 t/yr ([IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/e-waste-management-rules-2022-assessing-indias-extended-producer-responsibility-framework/); [Rajya Sabha PQ](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/758758/1/PQ_267_13022025_U1006_p167_p168.pdf)); other counts (295, 407, 472, about 384) differ by date and source, and no 2026 count was found. My inference is that the binding constraint is collection and enforcement, not capacity. Attero leads (over 30% certificate share claimed, FY25 revenue about Rs 961 cr at about 3% EBITDA, about Rs 8,300 cr capex plan; no IPO found) ([Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/entrepreneurship/news/e-waste-recycler-attero-scale-soars-22x-to-rs-961-cr-in-fy25-pat-remains-flat-9e54d5e0-1ebd-442a-a485-b1ff7db59b7e); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/attero-plans-to-invest-over-rs-8000-crore-in-5-years-to-ramp-up-e-waste-battery-recycling-capacity)); Karo Sambhav is a producer responsibility organisation, not a recycler. India has no mandate to recover refrigerants ([CEEW](https://www.ceew.in/node/3159)). No e-waste certificate-fraud case was found (the large fraud cases are plastic EPR). Refurbishers get a formal route: refurbishing certificates defer a producer's obligation by the extended product life ([GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)). Because deferral is not elimination and the price floor raises recycling cost, refurbishing becomes relatively more valuable to OEMs (inference); the certificate formula was not found.

Elsewhere, the EU's July 2025 evaluation found only about 40% of e-waste collected and recycled, and a WEEE revision was expected with the Circular Economy Act in H2 2026, possibly turning the Directive into a Regulation ([Valpak](https://www.valpak.co.uk/eu-weee-directive-revision-2026/)); **whether the proposal has been published by November 2026 was not confirmed**, and a possible EUR 2/kg charge on uncollected e-waste is unconfirmed advocacy-sourced material ([EEB](https://eeb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Policy-brief_Rewiring-Producer-Responsibility-for-E-Waste_2026.pdf)). China's 2026 trade-in scheme covers six categories at 15% for grade-1 efficiency with a 1,500 yuan cap ([Shanghai gov](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-Latest-WhatsNew/20251231/66a88a555a69487195e0d79444eee5d6.html)). Japan's retailer-collects, manufacturer-recycles, consumer-pays model has conflicting recycling-rate targets across METI, Wikipedia and older sources ([METI](https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/recycle/main/english/law/home.html)). US state e-waste and appliance EPR was not answered; the search returned only packaging EPR.

## AI and IoT reduce wasted visits, but outcome evidence is vendor-grade

The software layer is led by ServiceTitan, with FY2026 revenue of about USD 961M (+24%) and about 10,800 customers ([ServiceTitan IR](https://investors.servicetitan.com/news-releases/news-release-details/servicetitan-announces-fiscal-fourth-quarter-and-full-fiscal)); a Q2 FY2027 figure of USD 292.8M with raised guidance comes from a single aggregator and is unverified. Management says its AI product Max should roughly double subscription revenue per customer when ramped (a management claim) ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/03/12/servicetitan-ttan-q4-2026-earnings-transcript/)). It targets larger multi-technician firms while Housecall Pro and Jobber serve small shops; "best appliance repair software" rankings are mostly written by competitors such as Fieldproxy, whose 92-98% first-time-fix case studies are single-company marketing. Generic FSM market forecasts (about USD 5.1B in 2025 to USD 9.17B in 2030, MarketsandMarkets) are not appliance-specific.

The best-quantified AI evidence is not about appliances. Aquant's 2025 benchmark (about 160 service organisations, over 600,000 technician records) found AI-tool users resolved issues **39% faster** with 21% higher accuracy, and that 14% of truck rolls were unnecessary, but its sample was medical, industrial and printing equipment, and it is vendor-run and observational ([24x7 Magazine](https://24x7mag.com/maintenance-strategies/asset-management/third-party-repair/ai-enabling-39-faster-repairs-field-service-report-shows)). Other first-time-fix claims are vendor-only or untraceable: Bruviti's 25-35%, a McKinsey case study that was not located, GE SmartHQ's 95%, AR remote-assist figures (Vuzix, Zoho) with no controlled study, and Expansive FM's "high 80s to low 90s" predictive-maintenance figure repeated second-hand by trade press. A ServiceMag author cautions that connected data reflects what the control board believes and that most US appliances will need traditional repair for 10-15 years ([ServiceMag](https://www.servicemag.org/news/ai-predictive-maintenance-appliance-service)). Gartner predicted on 3 Feb 2026 that half of companies cutting service staff for AI will rehire by 2027 (headline only; the page returned 403) ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-03-gartner-predicts-half-of-companies-that-cut-customer-service-staff-due-to-ai-will-rehire-by-2027)). My reading: AI credibly cuts avoidable dispatches and triage time, while headline FTFR gains stay unaudited. No peer-reviewed study of computer-vision or audio fault detection, no BCG or McKinsey appliance work, and no measured agentic-dispatch outcomes were found.

OEM connectivity is built to capture service. Samsung expanded Home Appliance Remote Management globally in August 2025 (reported as 122 countries; via press summaries) ([e-commerce.news](https://e-commerce.news/story/samsung-expands-remote-appliance-service-to-122-countries-worldwide)). Per ServiceMag, Samsung routes error codes and pre-diagnosis to its certified network, GE SmartHQ Service is open to independents by subscription, LG's Smart Diagnosis decodes acoustic tones, and Whirlpool has no equivalent public professional app ([ServiceMag](https://servicemag.org/guides/connected-appliance-diagnostics-service-modes)). Whirlpool's added parts identification is single-trade-source. Samsung's self-repair programme (launched Aug 2022) reportedly ended its iFixit partnership, with no date found. 3D-printed parts remain pilots (Whirlpool with Spare Parts 3D assessed 150 parts; Miele via BASF), mostly in years-old coverage ([3DPrintingIndustry](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/whirlpool-to-introduce-3d-printing-to-appliance-aftersales-with-spare-parts-3d-143574/)). On workforce, BLS counts about 37,300 US appliance repairers (May 2024) with 2.6-3% growth to 2034, and a 10,000-15,000 shortage claim could not be traced to a primary source ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/Oes/current/oes499031.htm)). Forecasts cluster at 3-4.5% CAGR from bases of USD 30-68B (Wise Guy, MRFR, Spherical Insights), outliers of 9.5-9.8% look unreliable, and US appliance repair revenue is about USD 7B in 2025 (IBISWorld via ServiceMag) ([ServiceMag](https://servicemag.org/news/technician-workforce-shortage-2026)).

## Conclusion

The research changes one assumption: the money and the regulation are not where repair marketplaces would suggest. Underwriters (Asurion, Assurant, Frontdoor) are consolidating and growing, rental and recommerce firms in India turned profitable only after cutting cost ratios, and India's EPR price-floor litigation, not any right-to-repair statute, is the policy variable with the largest direct cost for appliance OEMs. Urban Company's Native line suggests the viable marketplace strategy is to own a hardware-plus-service annuity rather than to rent out technician labour.

The central uncertainty is evidentiary. Almost every growth, AI-uplift and market-size figure rests on a vendor, a syndicated report or a single secondary source, and several pivotal facts (EU formal notices to 18 states, Asurion's completion of D&G, Schedule III targets, the Delhi HC outcome, whether the EU WEEE proposal is out) should be confirmed against primary sources before any decision relies on them. Gaps that remain open across the notes: Asurion audited revenue; loss ratios and attach rates; Zopper and Onsitego financials; OEM service-network counts other than Samsung; the India installed base; Housejoy, Jeeves, Bundles and Refabd status; appliance refurbishment economics; recycler technology and refrigerant economics; per-category EPR prices; US state e-waste and appliance EPR; and any appliance-specific DPP date.
