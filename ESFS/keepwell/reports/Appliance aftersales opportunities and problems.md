# India's appliance after-sales gaps: money sits in B2B

As of November 2026, the best-evidenced money in household-appliance after-sales is business-to-business, not consumer-facing. It sits in protection and warranty underwriting, OEM-funded service and reverse logistics, and e-waste compliance. The best-evidenced consumer pain, meanwhile, has no obvious payer. Indian consumers hit warranty refusal, spare-part delay, overcharging and unreachable brand service. A self-selected LocalCircles poll found **55% of households had an inherently defective high-value product in warranty, and only 21% of those who acted got a refund or replacement** ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/industry/news/53-consumers-did-not-get-refund-for-inherently-defective-products-survey-124031500916_1.html)). Technicians are informal, income-volatile and poorly equipped, and independent shops depend on non-original parts. The circular economy leaks to scrap dealers while a Delhi High Court fight over the EPR certificate floor leaves producer costs uncertain. Four findings follow. First, the most credible revenue wedges for a new entrant are traceable buyback and reverse logistics tied to EPR and refurbishing certificates, OEM-facing triage and claims tooling, and institutional or B2B service. Second, the loudest consumer problems (warranty refusal, price opacity) are real but have unproven willingness to pay. Third, the safety, worker-welfare, refrigerant and open-data problems are public goods that need grants, regulation or OEM and EPR money. Fourth, protection distribution, on-demand repair marketplaces and field-service dispatch SaaS are crowded. Almost everything below is inferred from search-summary-level evidence, so each claim carries an evidence label, and the final section lists what to validate by interview or primary source.

## How to read the evidence labels

The upstream notes were built mostly from search-result summaries, with few pages opened in full. This report therefore uses five labels. **MEASURED** means an official statistic or a survey with a stated sample. **SELF-SELECTED** means large online polls (LocalCircles) that are not representative. **ANECDOTAL** means individual complaints, court cases or single posts. **VENDOR/SINGLE-SOURCE** means company claims, syndicated market estimates or one secondary outlet. **INFERENCE** means my own reasoning, not evidence. Market context established earlier is taken as given: protection earns the profit (Asurion agreed to buy Domestic & General for a reported GBP 2.1bn, a Bloomberg leak rather than an official figure ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2025/12/03/849663.htm)); Frontdoor guided to roughly a 27% adjusted EBITDA margin ([Frontdoor release](https://www.webull.com/news/15871082116127744))). Repair is fragmented and slow-growing, the EU Right to Repair Directive applied from 31 July 2026 with patchy transposition, India has no binding right to repair, and India's e-waste EPR regime is the most commercially consequential rule for appliance makers ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/directive-repair-goods_en)).

## Consumers suffer from refusal, cost and opacity, but few will pay to fix it

The consumer evidence is broad but weakly measured. Consumer durables ranked in the top five National Consumer Helpline sectors, with 49,066 disposed grievances in 2023, 77,873 in 2024 and 38,885 in 2025, against 440,185 for e-commerce in 2024. That series was reported through a search summary that itself flagged it for confirmation against the primary annexure ([Rajya Sabha PQ 267](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/759128/1/PQ_267_18032025_U1951_p192_p193.pdf)). The fall between 2024 and 2025 cannot be read as fewer problems, because reclassification is not ruled out (INFERENCE). Consumer commissions repeatedly rule against brands and dealers on refused warranty, spare-part shortages and repair delay. They rejected, for example, Godrej's "warranty subject to spare-parts availability" defence on an AC ([SupremeToday](https://supremetoday.ai/kerala-forum-slams-godrej-over-ac-warranty-failure-awards-compensation-2025121513171614e142)). A Delhi commission ordered Fujitsu General to refund Rs 1.14 lakh plus Rs 25k for a defective AC ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/consumer-cases/delhi-consumer-court-directs-fujitsu-general-to-refund-114-lakh-for-defective-ac-awards-25k-compensation-532250)). These are ANECDOTAL case-by-case outcomes. Awards of roughly Rs 5-25k are small against the time cost, so most owners probably never litigate (INFERENCE).

Price is the second axis. In a June 2022 LocalCircles poll of about 22,000 respondents, **76% of AC owners did not use manufacturer service, 65% of them citing cost**, and about two in three said brand service charged 50% or more above local vendors ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/76-indians-dont-get-acs-fixed-by-manufacturers-due-to-high-costs-bad-service-survey-shows/997052)). This is SELF-SELECTED. The informal alternative has no assurance: Toxics Link's five-city study (focus groups plus a 498-consumer survey, covered only secondarily) found over half of technicians use non-original parts, which causes frequent malfunction ([Counterview](https://www.counterview.net/2026/03/alarming-decline-in-indias-repair.html)). Platform repair draws ANECDOTAL complaints of forced gas refills, damage-then-charge and disputed warranty revisits on Urban Company ([ConsumerComplaints.in](https://www.consumercomplaints.in/urban-company-my-ac-is-damaged-by-urban-technician-c3014760)). These show grievance types, not rates. The "over 80% of electronics repairs are unorganised" claim is a media claim and UNVERIFIED ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/magazine/story/happily-ever-after-5330)). The upshot is a trust gap on both sides, with brand service too costly and the informal market unverifiable. No source measures the rate of overcharging, fake parts, no-shows or repeat failure (gap).

Safety sits behind these. India has **no national statistic on gas-geyser carbon-monoxide deaths**, and many are never linked to the heater ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/in-depth/beware-of-gas-geysers)). Recurring clusters are reported in Mysuru, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Puducherry (ANECDOTAL/NEWS) ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/2-sisters-die-due-to-lpg-leakage-in-gas-geyser-in-mysuru)). NCRB data via Dataful attribute 17.2% of 2024's 5,888 fire deaths to electrical short circuits, with no appliance attribution ([Dataful](https://insights.dataful.in/articles/nearly-6000-fire-accident-deaths-in-2024-despite-sharp-decline-in-accidents)).

## Supply-side pain gives tooling and credit niches, and a few public goods

The technician evidence is strong on platform unrest and weak on appliances. Urban Company's beauty partners have protested repeatedly since 2021 over commission, ratings-linked blocking and paid kits (REPORTED). The company cut its top commission slab to 25% after the October 2021 protest ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/entrepreneurship/news/story/urban-company-to-reduce-commissions-increase-earnings-for-gig-workers-after-facing-backlash-309359-2021-10-14)). The Urban Company RHP lists professional unrest, reclassification and platform bypass as risks, via secondary summaries ([HDFC Sky](https://hdfcsky.com/news/urban-company-ipo-opens-on-september-10-key-details-and-risks-from-rhp)). Whether appliance technicians face the same pain is a hypothesis, since no appliance-specific protest evidence was found. Evidence on the other side conflicts: Redseer (possibly industry-commissioned) says full-time gig workers net about 2.5x comparable workers, at about Rs 138 an hour versus Rs 54 ([Redseer](https://redseer.com/digests/india-gig-workers-2026-market-growth-insights/)). The Economic Survey counts 12 million gig workers with about 40% earning under Rs 15,000 a month ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/union-budget/story/india-has-12-million-gig-workers-2-of-total-workforce-economic-survey-513429-2026-01-29)). Appliance pay ranges (AC field technician Rs 18,000-30,000 a month) come from a weakly sourced trade article ([TimesTech](https://timestech.in/?p=379446)).

Skills and parts are the structural bottlenecks. Toxics Link reports informal training, outdated programmes with little smart-device exposure, thin diagnostic tools and original parts bottlenecked at authorised centres, with Hyderabad technicians citing manufacturer software restrictions (MEASURED focus groups, covered secondarily) ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/telangana/why-hyderabads-it-household-repair-shops-have-become-bad-at-fixing-your-devices-study)). A Blue Star listing demands inverter-AC PCB repair, IGBT replacement and oscilloscope diagnosis (a single ANECDOTAL posting) ([foundit](https://www.foundit.in/job/ssg-professional-blue-star-limited-ahmedabad-47494509)). The skills gap is therefore qualitatively supported and quantitatively unsupported. India's Right to Repair portal and proposed Repairability Index exist but are not statutory, and a 2026 status was not confirmed ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/govt-launches-right-to-repair-portal-to-promote-circular-economy-reduce-e-waste/1993903/)). Appliance-specific counterfeit evidence is absent (only auto-parts estimates were found), so any "fake parts" thesis for appliances is UNVERIFIED.

## AI, IoT and embedded finance are credible only where a business pays

Technology evidence is mostly vendor-grade. iFixit's FixBot launched 9 Dec 2025, free for now with a planned $4.99 a month tier, and its CEO admitted it "sometimes gets things wrong" ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/09/ifixit-launches-fixbot-ai-repair-helper-with-free-and-paid-versions/)). Bernard, Revin AI and Bruviti sell diagnosis to repair firms and OEM support teams, and Bernard's and Revin's funding figures are UNVERIFIED ([F4 Fund](https://f4.fund/startups/bernardhq)). No independent field accuracy figure exists for photo or sound diagnosis of appliances. Audio-fault results (about 93% on three compressor conditions at IIT Kanpur) come from small controlled datasets ([Bohrium](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/smartphone-application-for-fault-recognition/813084449246806018-53875)). The broader AI-uplift picture, from the earlier research, is that Aquant's 2025 benchmark showed 39% faster resolution but on medical, industrial and printing equipment, so it does not transfer cleanly to household appliances ([24x7 Magazine](https://24x7mag.com/maintenance-strategies/asset-management/third-party-repair/ai-enabling-39-faster-repairs-field-service-report-shows)). Non-intrusive load monitoring can flag gross faults such as a stuck-on fridge, but the authors say it is unclear whether signature fidelity supports precise fault detection ([Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/id/eprint/66609)).

Finance and agents follow the same pattern: the funded categories sell to businesses. Warranty distribution is funded in India (Servify, Onsitego, Zopper), and Polygrade raised $6.3M in 2026 to automate home-warranty claims for US carriers ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/toronto-software-startup-ploygrade-emerges-from-stealth-to-build-ai-for-home-warranty-claims/)). No source covers repair EMI, technician micro-loans or parametric warranty. A consumer-side claims agent was not found anywhere, which is an absence in a thin search and not proof of a gap. A digital product passport (DPP) registry is reportedly operational from 20 July 2026 ([Commission DPP page](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport_en)). My inference from the earlier research is that no appliance act binds before about 2028-29, and appliance timing conflicts across vendor sources ([RSM](https://www.rsm.global/belgium/en/node/554)).

## OEMs, retailers and institutions pay for compliance, claims and uptime

B2B is where the notes find real revenue and real pain. EU Directive 2024/1799 and India's E-Waste Rules 2022 both force OEMs to hold per-SKU parts, repairability and sales data that their ERPs often lack (INFERENCE). In India, the March 2024 amendment set the EPR certificate price band at 30%-100% of environmental compensation, and the widely cited floor is about Rs 22/kg. That figure is single-secondary-source and conflicts with a Rs 10-50/kg market range ([Zerodha Chatter](https://thechatter.zerodha.com/p/how-indias-e-waste-rules-found-their)). Large OEMs challenged the floor in the Delhi High Court, which stayed enforcement against one petitioner in January 2026, and no final judgment was found ([Newslaundry](https://www.newslaundry.com/amp/story/2026%2F01%2F14%2Fblue-star-gets-temporary-relief-as-delhi-hc-stays-regulators-e-waste-price-declaration)). Bajaj Electricals' EPR bill reportedly rose from about Rs 9.5 cr to about Rs 18 cr (UNVERIFIED). Refurbishing certificates defer a producer's obligation by the extended product life ([GreenSutra](https://greensutra.in/news/e-waste-epr-explained-2026/)). Recykal raised a reported $23M bridge in June 2026 and claims 650+ brands, which is company marketing with conflicting tracker totals ([Startupfeed](https://startupfeed.in/recykal-bridge-round-23-mn-bold-global-push/)).

Elsewhere the notes found documented but thinly priced demand. Rooftop-solar operations and maintenance is disorganised with thin economics, CEEW finds households hesitant to renew maintenance after five years, and owners report inverter-service delays and unclear warranty responsibility ([CEEW](https://www.ceew.in/node/4977)). EV-charger uptime figures conflict (71% operational per Bolt.Earth, 55-60% unsourced, 90-94% industry average) ([Bolt.Earth](https://bolt.earth/blog/indias-ev-charger-gap-only-71-public-ev-charging-stations-are-operational)). Returns and open-box resale technology has consolidated globally (Blue Yonder bought Optoro, completed 19 Aug 2025, per a competitor vendor's blog) ([ReverseLogix](https://www.reverselogix.com/compare/optoro/)). No evidence was found on housing-society, PG, hotel, hospital or commercial-kitchen AMC.

## Circular models work where control of sourcing, testing and warranty sits in one hand

Rental is the proven Indian circular model. Rentomojo's FY26 revenue was Rs 387 cr, with PAT of Rs 104.3 cr including a one-time Rs 36.6 cr tax credit. It reports a useful asset life of about 10 years, **2017 assets having earned 5.12x their cost**, and 56% still earning ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/rentomojos-post-ipo-test-can-the-flywheel-keep-going/)). Redseer sizes furniture-and-appliance rental at Rs 1,550 cr in 2025, rising to about Rs 6,030 cr by 2030 (VENDOR, with Rentomojo's organised share at 42-47%). Appliance-only rental economics, landlord leasing and low-income second-life markets were not found. Furlenco's FY25 figures (a reported Rs 240 cr revenue and Rs 3 cr profit per Tracxn via Mint) conflict with Entrackr's earlier-reported Rs 229 cr and Rs 3.1 cr, so treat them as UNVERIFIED ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/furlenco-turns-around-in-fy25-posts-profit-after-rs-130-cr-loss-revenue-surges-65-10586639)).

Refurbished large appliances are real but unproven at scale. Underdog (France, 2022) controls sourcing through sale and offers a two-year warranty with a EUR 99 five-year extension ([Underdog](https://underdog.shop/products/seche-linge-pompe-a-chaleur-9-kg-reconditionne-whirlpool-w7d94wbfr-bd657)). Whirlpool's certified-refurbished programme gives one year, and Back Market's third-party refurbishers give one year or more ([Whirlpool](https://www.whirlpool.com/refurbished-appliances.html)). Delhi's Costify reports 8,000+ customers, prices 50-70% below new, over Rs 4 crore annual revenue and ITI-trained technicians, with margins and warranty length undisclosed (company-sourced, via a single profile) ([Startuppedia](https://startuppedia.in/startup-stories/sibling-duo-from-delhi-that-started-a-refurbishment-startup-for-home-appliances-raise-funding-now-clocking-rs-4-cr-annual-revenue-10894704)). No source gives unit economics, return rates or warranty-claim rates for any appliance refurbisher. Warranty liability is therefore the largest unknown (INFERENCE). Costify says OEM partnerships are rare, and OEM returns and seconds stock is the cheapest feedstock (INFERENCE).

The leakage point is the informal buyback. India's e-waste is reportedly handled over 90% informally by one estimate, while Attero's CEO says the informal share fell from about 99% to 75% in four years, so sources conflict ([Context/TRF](https://www.context.news/just-transition/in-delhis-e-waste-hub-indias-informal-workers-lose-business)). Informal operators pay cash with no GST and no compliance cost, and so outbid formal recyclers ([Zerodha Chatter](https://thechatter.zerodha.com/p/how-indias-e-waste-rules-found-their)). Scrap-blog rates (fridge body Rs 18-40/kg plus compressor Rs 300-700; exchange offers Rs 2,000-5,000) are UNVERIFIED ([DigiRake](https://knowledge.digirake.com/refrigerator/sell-old-refrigerator-delhi)). Brand exchanges via Attero's Selsmart value units on the scrap route only (INFERENCE from the Attero-run valuation). That suggests a missing triage step that separates refurb-grade, harvest and scrap units at pickup (INFERENCE). The same touchpoint is the cheapest point to recover refrigerant. India has no effective recovery mandate, about 40% of refrigerant demand is servicing, and end-of-life gas is vented informally, per a trade source ([Refindustry](https://refindustry.com/news/market-research/current-status-and-barriers-to-lifecycle-refrigerant-management-in-india)). The 2023 E-Waste Rules amendment added end-of-life refrigerant management for room ACs, but CEEW says implementation is "yet to take up" (snippet-level). No adopted Article 5 carbon-credit pathway for HFC recovery was found.

The worker side is a high-impact, low-revenue problem. Reported figures (via secondary citations) include 76% of informal e-waste workers with respiratory ailments and 4-5 lakh children aged 10-15 in e-waste handling ([Zerodha Chatter](https://thechatter.zerodha.com/p/how-indias-e-waste-rules-found-their)). Seelampur workers earn Rs 200-800 a day and sit outside the Unorganised Workers' Social Security Act scheme through "legal invisibility" ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/connecting-the-unconnected/delhis-unregulated-e-waste-markets-and-the-marginalised-women-workers-who-bear-its-cost)). Formal expansion is squeezing their income without a transition plan ([Malay Mail](https://malaymail.com/news/money/2025/11/27/indias-e-waste-crackdown-is-growing-but-it-is-squeezing-the-workers-who-built-the-system/199751)). The fraud risk is documented for plastic EPR (about 7 lakh fake certificates per CSE/CPCB) but no e-waste fraud dataset was found ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/waste/70k-fake-certificates-major-polluters-missing-misuse-of-epr-legislation-worsening-indias-plastic-problem-shows-cse-report)). The government also cannot reliably verify claimed recycling ([Zerodha Chatter](https://thechatter.zerodha.com/p/how-indias-e-waste-rules-found-their)). Robotic disassembly is proven only for uniform products such as iPhones (Apple's Daisy, about 200 phones an hour) and is likely uneconomic for varied large appliances in India (INFERENCE) ([Ceramics.org](https://ceramics.org/ceramic-tech-today/apples-new-robot-daisy-recycles-high-quality-materials-from-up-to-200-devices-per-hour)).

## Ranked tiers: where revenue is plausible and where it is not

Ranking combines evidence strength, existing proof of money and a buyer who can pay. It is my judgment (INFERENCE), not a sourced ranking.

**Tier 1, big revenue potential.** The common thread is a business buyer and an existing line item. These are protection and warranty distribution (proven, with filings-based revenue at Servify and OneAssist and a global consolidation; but crowded); OEM-funded or EPR-funded reverse logistics with triage (a regulated spend line, but exposed to the Delhi HC outcome and to informal price competition); and OEM-facing triage, parts and claims tooling (pain asserted, uplift vendor-evidenced). Rental belongs here by profits, but it is dominated by incumbents and mostly furniture-led.

**Tier 2, niche revenue.** Refurbished large appliances (real operators, no public unit economics); a model-to-part graph and verified parts marketplace; vernacular technician copilots; technician upskilling and tool financing; shop SaaS; institutional AMC and solar or EV service; consumer claim agents; fair-price verification; and a service-history passport. Each has a plausible buyer but unproven willingness to pay, and several (shop SaaS, institutional AMC, credit) have no evidence at all.

**Tier 3, no revenue but high impact or public good.** Gas-geyser CO and appliance fire attribution (no data exists); informal-worker health and just transition; refrigerant recovery mandates; an open Indian repair dataset aligned to the Open Repair Alliance format (over 305,649 repairs in the global database per Wikipedia) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repair_Caf%C3%A9)); a third-party Repairability Index calculator and parts-price index; a crowd-sourced brand reliability score like Which? (UK fridge brand fault rates ranged 3% to 19% in 2020-21 data) ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/best-and-worst-large-appliance-brands-new-which-survey-results-revealed-aQufp1S1eeEw)); and an open manual and fault-code base. These could ride on grants, hackathon prizes, regulator contracts or EPR and OEM sponsorship, which are all INFERENCE.

## Crowded versus open: an inference from what the searches did not find

This section is entirely INFERENCE. An absence in a roughly 15-20-call search per note is weak evidence of an open market.

**Crowded.** Device and appliance protection distribution (Servify, Onsitego, Zopper, OneAssist, Croma ZipCare, with Servify's Series D3 of Rs 66.27 cr in March 2025 ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/servify-secures-inr-6627-cr-in-series-d3-funding-led-by/488839))). On-demand repair marketplaces (Urban Company, ServiceOnDoors, Eazzy, Flipkart's reported AC entry; quick-service players Snabbit and Pronto were not found offering appliance repair). Field-service dispatch SaaS and voice booking (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Probook at $40M, Avoca), mostly US and not appliance-specific. EPR certificate trading in India (Recykal, Attero). Returns and open-box technology, which has consolidated.

**Open or thin.** Independent-technician parts mapping with price transparency; board-level repair and donor-board exchange; vernacular, offline-first technician copilots; low-commission lead-gen for AC technicians; technician credit and insurance; a neutral fair-price verifier; a consumer-side claim agent; SKU-level traceability and refurbishing-certificate workflows for OEMs; triage at buyback; large-appliance claims triage and repair-network SLAs; institutional AMC; and large-appliance returns grading. Evidence is mixed on how "open" these are: Eazzy's and Wiffy's stated AI plans, Costify's OEM talks and Zopper's partner claims show attempts are underway even where no funded leader was found.

**Structural cautions.** Disintermediation is the main marketplace risk (Urban Company's RHP lists it, and Homejoy is the cautionary case, per secondary sources). Platform-bypass also threatens any intermediary built on one-off appliance repair, which is episodic (INFERENCE). Global context cuts both ways: EU law and subsidies (France's QualiRépar, Austria's contested successor scheme) create paid repair markets, whereas India's equivalents are voluntary or only proposed. A US technician workforce of about 37,300 appliance repairers (BLS, May 2024) shows how small the formal labour pool is ([ServiceMag](https://servicemag.org/news/technician-workforce-shortage-2026)). The 10,000-15,000 shortage figure is UNVERIFIED.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing from "who will fix appliances" to "who pays when repair, resale and recycling are fixed". The consumer problems with the strongest evidence (warranty refusal, cost gap, price opacity) lack a payer, while the problems with a payer (EPR, claims, OEM compliance, rental) sit mostly in B2B. The most interesting position is at the physical touchpoint where an old appliance leaves a home. A pickup that triages the unit, prices the compressor and copper fairly, captures refrigerant and records provenance serves the consumer, the refurbisher, the recycler and the EPR obligation at once. That position rests largely on inference and on the unresolved Delhi High Court price-floor outcome, so interviews with OEM sustainability, EPR and service heads are the first test.

The second implication is that Tier 3 and Tier 1 are linked. Worker transition, certificate integrity and refrigerant recovery are public goods, but they are also the traceability and compliance layer that makes EPR-funded revenue credible. A venture that builds the traceability once can serve both, whereas pure consumer apps for repair information or diagnosis face weak willingness to pay and platform-bypass risk. India's evidence base remains thin on technicians, shops, institutions and underserved households, and that thinness is the main limit on this report.
