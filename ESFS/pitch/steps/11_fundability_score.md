## 11.1 How this step is built
This uses the course's own matrix (M3): **Market (15), Product / Service (30), Startup (45)**, each scored on **Potential, Uniqueness and Quality**, plus an **X-factor (10)**, for 100 points in total.

Scoring rule, from our Class Activity 4: *"a full score is earned by verifiable achievements, not by describing intentions."* So:
- A cell resting on **[Story]** facts can't get full marks.
- Every cell names the evidence and **what it would take to score higher**.

We scored MediQuick **42/100** with the same rule. The benchmark in the course's Scorecard example is **80**.

**Reading:**
- **78 is just under the course benchmark of 80**, and far above MediQuick's 42 under the same strict rule. That is a fair place for a Stage 2 company with 9 months of paid usage but only 14 agencies.
- **Valuation effect.** Scorecard post-money = ₹24.77 Cr × 78 ÷ 80 = **₹24.1 Cr**. With the comparable (₹24.8 Cr) and VC-method (₹24.2 Cr) values, the pitched pre-money is **₹19 Cr**, capped at the VC-method price (Step 9).
- **The two weakest cells are Product–Uniqueness and Product–Quality** (both 7/10). The deck must defend them with the *learning curve* and the *data moat*, not with feature lists.
