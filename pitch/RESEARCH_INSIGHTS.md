# Keepwell: what the research taught us (17–19 Nov 2026)

This file keeps the lessons from two strategy explorations that were archived. Full files, data and dashboard are in [`archive/`](archive/):
- `archive/STRATEGY_REBUILD_v2_care_verify.md` and `strategy_model_v2.py`
- `archive/market_intel/` (report, methodology, 10 CSVs, a 9-page dashboard, raw research by 5 agents with sources)

## The verdict on each direction

| Direction | Verdict | Why, in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit as an agency tool at ₹40 per fix (the original pitch) | Too small as priced; value not proven | Agencies dislike paying for software, and the savings (mentor estimate: ~₹30k a month for a 5-technician agency) were never measured |
| Keepwell Care: a ₹1,499 plan sold by agencies | **Rejected** | The agency can sell its own AMC and keep all ₹1,499. Extended warranties already cover the first 2–5 years |
| Keepwell Verify: brands pay ₹20 per verified job | **Rejected** | In-warranty work goes to brand service centres, not our agencies. The proof comes from the agency's own technician, and brands already have apps for it |
| Retailer-led post-warranty repair routing | **Not viable as is** | About ₹56 contribution per job (BASE); break-even needs about 14,000 jobs a month from about 1,100 retailers. Easy for incumbents to copy |

## Insights worth keeping

**Market size (Tier 2/3, 449 cities, BASE)**
- About 5.7 Cr installed ACs, refrigerators, washing machines and purifiers, of which about 4.9 Cr are out of warranty.
- About 2.5 Cr paid service and repair jobs a year, worth about ₹2,200 Cr. The range is ₹560 Cr (LOW) to ₹9,900 Cr (HIGH).
- AC jobs are most of the value. Purifier filter visits are most of the count.
- The average bill is about ₹900. AC service is about ₹550, a gas refill about ₹2,300, and a repair about ₹1,200–1,500.

**Who does the work today** (AC service, LocalCircles 2026, n = 13,487)
- **44% local independent technicians.** This is the biggest and least organised share.
- **24%** go to the brand.
- **13%** are arranged by the dealer.
- **13%** go through a platform.

**Where the money already flows**
- Retailers earn from extended warranties. Electronics Mart made about ₹12 Cr of revenue and about ₹4 Cr of profit from warranties in one quarter.
- Onsitego, OneAssist, Zopper, resQ, Jeeves and ZipCare hold the retail counters.
- Brands keep post-warranty service. Eureka Forbes earns about 31% of its revenue from service, and LG runs 1,006 service centres.
- Urban Company keeps 28.6% of partner earnings. A partner nets about 49% of the job value. Over 90% of its value comes from its top 10 cities, so it is thin in Tier 2/3.

**Nobody has organised the informal technician market in Tier 2/3.** Housejoy, Taskbob, Doormint and Pync failed or pivoted. Bigfix and SERVIZ moved to B2B work.

**Best pilot geography on evidence:** Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Bajaj Electronics has 178 public stores there and Sathya has 65 in AP. The team is also based there. The cities are Warangal, Vijayawada, Visakhapatnam, Guntur and Karimnagar. The backup is Indore, Raipur and Bhopal.

**Biggest unknowns**
- Jobs per agency per day. Our own figures range from 2 to 15.
- Repair frequency per machine. No Indian survey exists, so US data is used.
- Whether fewer repeat trips actually make an agency money.

## Lessons for the next decision
1. **Don't keep changing who pays.** Agencies, then plans and brands, then retailers: each failed for the same reason. We were a middleman with nothing the others couldn't do themselves.
2. **The only thing we own is Circuit:** preparing the job so it's fixed in one visit, and learning from every verified fix. Start from there.
3. **Measure before modelling.** Talk to 10 real agencies and ask:
   - How many calls and jobs do you get a day?
   - How many trips are repeat trips?
   - Do you sell AMCs?
   - Are you a brand service centre?
   - What do you pay Justdial or Urban Company?
   - What is a saved trip worth to you?
