### 2.6 Pre-seed: iSAFE from operator angels vs friends and family (analysed with later rounds in mind)
**First, the terms.** "Friends and family" is a *source* of money. iSAFE is an *instrument*. So there are really three options:

| | A. Friends and family, priced equity | B. Friends and family, iSAFE | C. Operator angels, iSAFE (draft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical cheque (course M2) | ₹1–10L each, so ₹30L means **3–10 people** | same | ₹10L–1Cr each, so ₹30L means **2 people** |
| What it signals to a seed VC | Founders' own network believes in them. No outside validation | same | **People who know the industry checked us and put in money.** Course M1: in the "Finding a Business Model" stage, *strategic angels* bring "industry access" and "introduce pilot customers" |
| Valuation needed now? | **Yes.** A price is set before any pilot data: too low gives away equity, too high makes the seed look like a down round | No | No |
| Cap table at seed | Many small **voting** equity holders. Each must sign the seed shareholders' agreement (SSHA) and waive pre-emptive rights, and is bound by drag-along | Few holders, but unsophisticated ones holding a convertible they may not understand | 2 holders who sign quickly |
| Relationship risk | High (course M2: "risks to relationships", "high emotional involvement") | High | Low |
| Value beyond money | Goodwill | Goodwill | Mentoring, pilot introductions, credibility with the seed lead |

**What iSAFE is in India.** A US-style SAFE contract isn't recognised under Indian company law. The iSAFE (popularised by 100X.VC) is issued as **CCPS**: compulsorily convertible preference shares. Its terms say it converts at the next priced round at the lower of a valuation cap or a discount.

What it costs to issue, all one-time and handled by our CS/CA (Step 10):
- a registered-valuer report
- private-placement filings (PAS-4 offer letter, special resolution, PAS-3 return of allotment)
- shareholder approval

Two things are now easier:
- **"Angel tax"** under Section 56(2)(viib) was abolished from FY 2024-25 by the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024. Issuing above fair value no longer creates a tax problem. DPIIT-recognised startups were already exempt.
- **Course M2 market data:** SAFEs and similar are under 2% of deals, ticket ₹10–50L, used in early seed. So it is used, but it is not the default.

**Does an iSAFE complicate later rounds? Yes, in five specific ways. Each one is fixable in the term sheet now.**

> **Note on the numbers below.** All figures below are the **final Step 9 numbers**: seed price ₹113.33 at ₹20 Cr pre-money, cap price ₹64.67.

| # | Complication at seed / Series A | Our numbers (from the model) | How we design it out now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Conversion dilution lands on the founders.** Seed VCs require notes and the ESOP top-up to convert *before* their money, inside the pre-money | Cap price ₹64.67 vs seed price ₹113.33: the angels get ~1.75x more shares than the seed investor per rupee, 3.0% before the seed (2.1% after) for ₹30L | Use a **post-money cap**: the angels' 3.0% is fixed and known today. It doesn't grow if the seed is larger |
| 2 | **Liquidation-preference overhang.** If the iSAFE converts into seed preference shares and the preference is set at the seed price, the angels' 1x preference becomes worth more than they paid | 46,392 shares × ₹113.33 = **₹52.6L of preference for ₹30L invested** | Preference on the converted shares is **capped at 1x the amount actually invested** (₹30L). This is a "shadow series" with identical rights apart from the preference amount |
| 3 | **Stacking.** Several notes at different caps make the cap table hard to read, and VCs discount messy cap tables (course M3: "Cap table (no surprises)") | 1 note | **One iSAFE round, one cap, closed.** No rolling notes before the seed |
| 4 | **Anchoring.** A low cap can anchor the seed price | Cap ₹10 Cr post vs the seed's ₹24.75 Cr post | The cap reflects pre-pilot risk. The seed is priced on pilot results (Step 9). A ~2.5x step-up in ~6 months is a good signal ("the price went up because the risk came down") |
| 5 | **Rights creep.** Notes that carry board seats, vetoes, most-favoured-nation clauses or broad pro-rata rights tie the hands of the seed lead | — | **No board seat, no vetoes.** Information rights only. Pro-rata right for the seed round only. Most-favoured-nation clause ends at the seed. Conversion triggers on a priced round of ≥ ₹1 Cr, and long-stop conversion at the cap after 24 months |

**Does priced friends-and-family equity avoid these?** Only partly. It avoids notes, but brings a different set of later-round problems:
- a valuation set with no data
- several small voting shareholders, each of whom must sign every future shareholders' agreement and waiver
- each of them is a counterparty in drag-along and tag-along

The more people on the register, the slower every future round closes.

**Recommendation: keep option C, operator angels on a clean iSAFE, with the five protections above. Put the family money inside the founders' contribution.**

Why:
1. **The story is stronger.** "Two people who ran service networks backed us before the pilot" is outside validation from the industry. The course rewards exactly that at Stage 2 (strategic angels, pilot access).
2. **The cap table is cleaner for the seed and later rounds.** Two holders with a fixed 3.0% stake, no stacking, and no preference overhang.
3. **No early valuation.** We aren't stuck with a number set before the pilot.
4. **The personal story is kept.** Families backed the founders personally, and that is told as part of the founders' ₹15L (2.4). This holds the course's friends-and-family "relationship trust" beat with no cap-table cost.
