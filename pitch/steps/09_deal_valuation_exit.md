## 9.2 Who we raise from: one lead seed VC for the whole round
**₹4.75 Cr from a single lead seed VC (an early-stage VC, per M2), in one class of Seed CCPS.**

**Why one investor, not a syndicate:**
1. **Ownership a lead wants.** A lead seed VC usually targets 15–20% to justify a board seat and the work. One lead takes the full **20.0%**. Splitting it would leave the lead at ~11–12%, and good funds may then decline to lead.
2. **A clean cap table for later rounds** (Step 2 rule). One new holder instead of four or five, so every future agreement and waiver is signed faster.
3. **The cheque size is normal.** ₹4.75 Cr (~$0.5M) is an ordinary solo seed cheque. It sits within M2's early-VC range (₹1–100 Cr).
4. **The strategic value still comes in, through the ESOP pool, not the cap table.** The ex-warranty-administrator, the parts distributor and the ex-OEM service head join as **advisors on ESOP grants from the 10% pool** (Step 2.2).
5. **Existing iSAFE angels** may take their small pro-rata (~₹10L) inside the round. They're already on the cap table, so this adds no new holders.

**The real risks of a single investor, and our answers:**
- **Signalling risk:** if the only VC doesn't invest again at Series A, others read it badly. *Answer:* ask the lead to commit to reserves for its Series A pro-rata.
- **No price competition inside the round.** *Answer:* run a process with several funds competing to lead.

**Fallback:** if the chosen lead wants a smaller cheque (₹3.5–4 Cr), fill the rest with **one** co-investor. We never go below the **₹4.0 Cr minimum close** (Step 8).

## 9.4 Valuation: three course methods **[Model]**
**Why not price on revenue alone.** At ~₹7L annualised revenue, a revenue multiple gives a meaningless number. Following M4 ("pre-fundraising: Scorecard + Comparable"), we anchor on **comparable seed rounds** and **check** against the investor's required return. The VC method uses the **funded plan (Step 7.7, G)**, which matches the dilution the cap table assumes.

| Method | How | Post-money |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Comparable** | Average of: **Hisabkitab** (AI accounting for small firms, seed, ₹20 Cr); **Unveilr AI** (pre-seed, ₹16.7 Cr); **Indian seed-market midpoint** ($2–8M → ~$4M ≈ ₹37.6 Cr, Kae Capital) **[web]** | **₹24.8 Cr** |
| **2. Scorecard** | Comparable × (our score ÷ benchmark) = 24.8 × **78** ÷ 80 (Step 11) | **₹24.1 Cr** |
| **3. VC method** | ₹4.75 Cr at a **35% target IRR over 6 years** (M4: 35–40%; M2: early-VC horizon 5–8 years) → the investor needs ₹28.8 Cr. **Y6 revenue ₹30.8 Cr** (Y5 ₹19.2 Cr + 60%) × **7x** (Servify ≈ ₹5,660 Cr ÷ ₹781 Cr ≈ 7.2x **[repo]**) = **₹215.5 Cr exit**. Needs 13.3% at exit, so 19.6% today after Series A and B dilution (32%) | **₹24.2 Cr** |
| **Average** | | **₹24.4 Cr** |

**What we pitch: ₹19 Cr pre-money → ₹23.75 Cr post → 20.0% to the lead VC, at ₹107.51 per share.**
- **The rule:** the average of the methods, but **never above the VC-method price**. So the investor's base case clears the 35% hurdle.
- **The investor's base case:** their 20.0% becomes 13.6% after Series A and B. At a ₹215.5 Cr exit that's ₹29.3 Cr: **6.2x, ~35% IRR**.
- **On revenue:** ₹23.75 Cr post is ~34x annualised revenue. That's normal for a seed round, which is priced on comparable rounds and on the investor's required return, not on today's revenue.

### Negotiation plan (Class Activity 2 style)
| | Pre-money | Lead's stake | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anchor = VC-method ceiling (what we pitch)** | **₹19 Cr** | 20.0% | At the investor's own 35% hurdle |
| Investor's likely opening | ~₹15–16 Cr | ~23–24% | The comparables' low end |
| **Walk-away floor** | **₹16 Cr** | ~22.9% | Still inside the 15–25% norm; founders ~44% after Series B |

**Our arguments for holding at ₹19 Cr:**
1. **It is the investor's own maths.** At ₹19 Cr their base case returns 35%.
2. **9 months of paid traction.** 14 agencies, ~1.4% churn, the accuracy gate passed, the price tested.
3. **The upside isn't in the base case:** Tier 1, R6–R8, price reviews.
4. **The credits:** ~₹33L of AI and cloud cost paid by Google if approved.

**What we can trade instead of price:**
- the ESOP pool at 12% instead of 10%
- a small tranche (e.g. ₹0.75 Cr) released at the 120-agency gate

**Timeline:**

## 9.8 Weak points, said before an investor says them
| Weak point | Answer |
|---|---|
| "₹23.75 Cr post is ~34x your revenue" | Seed rounds are priced on comparable rounds and on the investor's required return. At ₹19 Cr pre, your own VC maths returns 35% |
| "Which line takes you past ₹100 Cr?" | Tier 1, plans and claims at record scale reach ~₹66 Cr by year 8; ₹100 Cr+ needs price reviews and the next categories. We don't overclaim |
| "Founders under 50% after Series B" | 46.2% at a 20% Series A; a 2–3x step-up keeps it nearer 50% |
| "Exit depends on a 7x multiple" | The conservative case is shown openly (5x, ₹110 Cr, ~21% IRR) |
| "Why only one investor?" | Clean ownership for the lead, fewer holders for later rounds; strategic value comes in via ESOP advisors; the lead reserves for Series A |
| "The growth plan needs a Series A" | Both plans do. Spending it on field sales needs *less* outside cash than staying lean (Step 7.7) |
