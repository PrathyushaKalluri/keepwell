## 9.1 How this step is built

## 9.3 Instrument: a priced seed round in CCPS
| Option | What later rounds would see | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Seed CCPS (compulsorily convertible preference shares), priced** | The Indian VC standard (course M2). Sets one clean price **now that pilot data exists**. **Converts the existing iSAFE**, so the cap table is clean before Series A. FDI-compatible if a foreign fund joins (issue at or above fair value per a registered valuer) | **Recommended** |
| A second iSAFE | **Stacking**: two notes at different caps, which Step 2 rules out. Delays the valuation question to Series A with more holders | No |
| Ordinary equity | Investors get no downside preference, so VCs won't accept it | No |
| Convertible debentures (CCD) | Debt until it converts; interest and conversion complexity | No |
| SISFS debt tranche / venture debt / bank loan | Needs repayment from revenue we don't yet have (course M2); venture debt only after Series A | No (Step 3) |

**How the iSAFE converts at the seed** (Step 2 protections, now in real numbers):
- **Cap binds.** The cap price is ₹64.67 per share, against a seed price of **₹113.33** (₹90.66 with the 20% discount). So the angels convert at **₹64.67**, into **46,392 shares (2.1% after the seed)**.
- **Preference capped at ₹30L,** the amount they actually invested. Uncapped, the converted shares would carry **₹52.6L** of preference (46,392 × ₹113.33). The ~₹22.6L overhang is removed by a "shadow series" with identical rights apart from the preference amount.

## 9.4 Valuation: three course methods **[Model]**
**Why not price on revenue multiples alone.** With Y2 revenue under ₹1 Cr, any revenue multiple gives a meaningless number. So, following M4 ("pre-fundraising planning: Scorecard + Comparable"), we anchor on **comparable seed rounds** and **check** against the investor's required return (the VC method). The VC method uses the **funded plan (Step 7.7, G)**, because its Series A and B dilution is already counted.

| Method | How | Post-money |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Comparable** | Average of three seed comparables: **Hisabkitab** (AI accounting for small firms, seed, ₹20 Cr); **Unveilr AI** (pre-seed, ₹16.7 Cr); the **Indian seed-market midpoint** ($2–8M post-money, so ~$4M ≈ ₹37.6 Cr, Kae Capital) **[web]** | **₹24.8 Cr** |
| **2. Scorecard** | Comparable baseline × (our score ÷ benchmark score) = 24.8 × **76** ÷ 80 (Step 11) | **₹23.5 Cr** |
| **3. VC method** | ₹4.75 Cr at a **35% target IRR over 6 years** (course M4: 35–40%; M2: early-VC horizon 5–8 years) → investor needs ₹28.8 Cr. Y6 revenue ₹34.8 Cr (Y5 ₹21.7 Cr + 60%) × **7x** (Servify ≈ ₹5,660 Cr ÷ ₹781 Cr ≈ 7.2x **[repo]**) = **₹243.5 Cr exit**. Needs 11.8% at exit, so 17.4% today after Series A and B dilution (32%) | **₹27.4 Cr** |
| **Average** | | **₹25.2 Cr** |

**What we pitch:**
- **₹20 Cr pre-money → ₹24.75 Cr post-money → the seed investors get 19.2%.**
- **Seed price: ₹113.33 per share.**
- 19.2% sits inside the Indian seed norm of **15–25% dilution** **[web]**.

**The investor's return at our price** (the VC-method check):
- Their 19.2% becomes 13.1% after Series A and B.
- At the ₹243.5 Cr exit that's ₹31.8 Cr: **6.7x money, ~37% IRR**.
- **That clears the 35% seed hurdle** (course M4). Our price is *below* the VC-method valuation (₹27.4 Cr post), so the investor's own maths supports it.

### Negotiation plan (Class Activity 2 style)
| | Pre-money | Seed stake | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| VC-method ceiling | ~₹22.6 Cr | ~17.4% | The investor's required-return maths |
| **Anchor (what we pitch)** | **₹20 Cr** | 19.2% | Average of the three methods |
| Investor's likely opening | ~₹16–17 Cr | ~22–23% | The comparables' low end |
| **Walk-away floor** | **₹16 Cr** | ~22.9% | Still inside the 15–25% norm. Below it, the founders fall under 45% after Series B |

**Our arguments for holding near ₹20 Cr:**
1. **The investor's own VC maths gives ₹27.4 Cr post.** We're asking for less.
2. **The pilot has already de-risked the round:** paid usage, value ~1.8–2.2x the fee, and a visible learning curve (Step 6).
3. **The upside isn't in the base case:** Tier 1, R6–R8, price reviews.
4. **The credits:** ~₹34L of AI and cloud cost paid by Google if approved.

**Concessions we can trade instead of price:**
- the ESOP pool at 12% instead of 10%
- a small tranche (e.g. ₹0.75 Cr) released at the 120-agency gate
- a board observer for the second fund

## 9.7 Exit strategy
**Who would buy Keepwell, and why** (repo competitor and M&A research):

| Acquirer type | Examples | What they'd be buying | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warranty / protection platforms** (most likely) | Servify, OneAssist, Onsitego, Zopper; global: **Asurion** | The machine record (prices plans, proves claims), the independent-agency network (service reach in Tier 2/3), claims-triage evidence | **Asurion bought Domestic & General** for a reported £2.1bn; Onsitego bought Qdigi (Feb 2024); Servify bought Jubi.ai **[repo]** |
| **Home-services platforms** | Urban Company | Tier 2/3 supply it doesn't have; agency software | Urban Company IPO (103.6x subscribed) **[repo]** |
| **Recommerce, refurbishment, recycling** | Cashify, Attero, Recykal | Verified history that raises resale value; EPR evidence | Quikr bought Zefo for ~₹200 Cr **[repo]**; Cashify IPO planned for early 2027 |
| **Field-service software (global)** | ServiceTitan-type players entering India | A ready Indian vernacular agency base | ServiceTitan $961M revenue **[repo]** |
| OEMs (less likely) | — | Repo: OEMs tend to "capture service into their own networks", so their interest is in data, not the company | — |

**Timeline and path:**
- **Strategic sale in years 6–8** to a warranty platform or a home-services company, once the record and claims lines are proven (that's the 6-year VC-method horizon).
- **IPO** only if the platform reaches Indian listed-SaaS scale. The Urban Company and Cashify listings show the route exists, but we don't promise it.
- **What makes us acquirable:** something no acquirer can build quickly, namely verified repair outcomes plus a record that follows the machine (Step 5 moat).
