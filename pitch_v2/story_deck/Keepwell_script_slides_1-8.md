## Slide 1: Keepwell keeps the promise the retailer made (Prathyusha, 0:34)

**On screen:** the logo, the line, the tagline, and three founder cards, each with a background line and a "Why" quote.

**Say:**
> Every local appliance retailer makes a promise at the counter: this will be installed properly, and it will be looked after. Then the sale ends, and nobody keeps that promise. Keepwell does. We are the after-sale service arm of the local retailer.
> We're three founders, and each of us saw this from one side. I'm Prathyusha: I spent ten years in appliance distribution, supplying six hundred local retailers, and watched them blamed for installs they never did. Kavyanth ran a brand's service network. Sri Charan built claims platforms.

**Justify:**
- **"The after-sale service arm of the local retailer":** "local retailer" means every independent appliance retailer in Tier 2/3 towns, from a single counter to a large showroom. National chains are excluded because they run their own service.
- **Founder backgrounds (Step 2):**
  - **Prathyusha:** 10 years in distribution, regional head for AP/TS, 600+ local retailers. A Guntur retailer showed her a consumer-court notice naming him beside the brand.
  - **Kavyanth:** 9 years in after-sales, zonal service head, about 220 authorised service centres; he knows the owners of 200+.
  - **Sri Charan:** voice AI, then engineering lead at a device-protection and claims platform.
- **"Why these founders?"** Each brings a network the business needs on day one: retailers, service centres, and the claims and plan product. Warm introductions cut the cost of signing a retailer from ₹22k to ₹9.5k.

## Slide 2: Retailers sell the appliance, then lose the customer (Prathyusha, 0:36)

**On screen:** three problem cards, then the result line on the click.

**Say:**
> Three things go wrong after the sale. Installs are slow: brands take two to five days, and fifty-one of the sixty-four retailers we met get the blame. Repairs are guesswork: most machines are out of warranty, and forty-four percent of AC service goes to local mechanics, with no fixed price and no record. And the retailer earns nothing after the sale, yet courts hold him liable.
> **[click]** So the person the family trusts has no way to keep the promise he made.

**Justify:**

| Claim | Source |

| 51 of 64 retailers get the blame | Our discovery interviews, Apr–Jul 2025 (Step 6) |
| Most machines are out of warranty | 80–91% of installed units (industry data, Step 4) |
| 44% of AC service goes to local mechanics | LocalCircles survey, 13,487 respondents |
| The retailer earns nothing after the sale | Independent retailers net 2–3% on the sale |
| Courts hold the retailer liable | Consumer commissions have held sellers jointly liable: Chennai ₹35k, Hyderabad ₹60k |

**If they ask "how many households?"** 7 of the 12 households we met keep no service record at all.

## Slide 3: The retailer sells the promise. Keepwell keeps it. (Kavyanth, 0:38)

**On screen:** the vision in one sentence, then Live / Next / Later on the click.

**Say:**
> I'm Kavyanth. Our answer is simple: the retailer sells the promise, and Keepwell keeps it. We look after every appliance a local retailer sells, for its whole life.
> **[click]** We build it in three steps. Today we serve: authorised installs in twenty-four hours, guaranteed repairs, maintenance plans, and Keepwell Protect, a two-year repair plan, all under the retailer's name. From year two we supply genuine parts and run partner clusters. From year three, the record verifies installs for brands, and old units get a second life.

**Justify:**
- **Authorised installs:** we install under sub-contracts with two brand-authorised service centres, so the warranty stays valid. Several brands void the warranty if anyone unauthorised installs.
- **24-hour installs:** 87% of pilot installs were done within 24 hours (Step 6).
- **Guaranteed repairs:** 90 days. 81% of repairs were fixed on the first visit.
- **Keepwell Protect** (₹2,000 for 2 years) is a repairs-only service contract we deliver ourselves, not insurance. Claims money is kept separate, and the formal legal opinion is due by month 6.
- **The order:** Serve (now) → Supply (year 2) → Know and circulate (year 3). Each step runs on the record the step before creates.

## Slide 4: Not every appliance reaches every stage (Sri Charan, 1:25)

**On screen:**
- Seven stage arrows across the top.
- **① Who reaches it:** ● every unit or ○ only some.
- **② What happens:** the first touch, the order of touches and what Keepwell does.
- **[click] ③ Value for each party:** Family, peace of mind · Retailer, retention · Technician, steady income · Brand or partner, reach and proof.
- **[click]** Three decisions: it breaks after warranty? worth repairing? can it be resold?

**Say:**
> I'm Sri Charan. This is the most important slide: one appliance's whole life, from the shop counter to the recycler. Every unit is sold, installed and lives through its warranty. After that, only some are covered, repaired, traded in or recycled. At each stage a different party makes the first touch: the family walks into the shop, we confirm the install, we send the month-eleven reminder, the family sends a voice note when it breaks. And at every stage we do one job: open the record, install in a day, remind, repair with a genuine part, collect, recycle.
> **[click]** Now read down any column, because this is why it works. The family gets peace of mind: one number, a fixed price and a guarantee. The retailer gets retention: a commission on every job, and the next sale when the old unit comes back. The technician gets steady income: paid within forty-eight hours, and busy even in the off-season. Brands and partners get reach and proof: installs in towns they can't staff, and a verified record.
> **[click]** Three questions decide where each appliance goes next: does it break, is it worth repairing, can it be resold. Every answer keeps it with us.

**Justify the value propositions:**

| Party | Value | Backing |
|---|---|---|
| Family | Peace of mind | One number on WhatsApp; installed in a day with the warranty valid; a fixed price upfront and a 90-day guarantee; exchange credit at the end of life |
| Retailer | Retention | 5% on installs, 8% on repairs, 12% on AMC, 25% on Protect; no blame for delays; the trade-in brings the next sale. 33 of 34 pilot retailers stayed |
| Technician | Steady income | Paid per job within 48 hours; AMC visits fill the off-season; the right part on the first visit. 1 of 22 pilot technicians left |
| Brand or partner | Reach and proof | Service centres get installs done in towns they can't staff; brands get a verified install and serial number; distributors get one pooled order; brands meet their e-waste take-back target |

**If they ask…**
- **"Why do stages 6 and 7 earn money?"** They are upside from year 3 and are not in the plan's numbers.
- **"What is the 'record'?"** One consented record per appliance, kept on WhatsApp under DPDP rules. It is what lets brands verify installs and claims later.

**If they ask…**

- **"How long does ₹18 L last?"** Head office and preparation for clusters 2–3 cost about ₹5 L a month net. That leaves about ₹2 L at a March 2027 close, plus the ₹5 L grant balance.

| On the slide | The arithmetic |

| 2,340 jobs | 195 × 12 |
| ₹28.1 L job costs (74%) | Technicians, parts, retailer commission, travel and payments. Per ₹100: 25 + 33 + 7 + 9 = 74 (slide 8) |
| ₹9.9 L contribution (26%) | ₹38.03 L × 26%. The same as 195 × ₹5,070 |

| The two loss months | March −₹39k, April −₹24k: fewer than 20 retailers |

**If they ask…**
- **"Is this the whole company's profit?"** No. It is the cluster's profit, before head office. Head office is in the plan: ₹0.40 Cr in year 1. The company is still EBITDA-positive in year 1 (slide 9).
- **"Do retailers stay?"** All 34 signed 2-year exclusive agreements, and 33 are still active.
- **"Is 12 jobs per retailer realistic?"** Pilot retailers sell about 74 appliances a month, from their ledgers. 12 jobs is about one job for every six appliances sold, and an AC needs an install.
- **"Where is Protect?"** It was sold only in the last 8 weeks (72 plans, a 6% attach at 12 retailers). It is booked over its 2-year term, so it is not in these numbers. It starts in year 1.

## Slide 7: Each retailer leaves ₹5,070 a month today and ₹9,600 at maturity (Kavyanth, 0:35)

**On screen:**
- **Top row:** the 12 jobs.
- **Three panels:** one retailer's month in the pilot, the same retailer in year 5, and one cluster's month.

**Say:**
> Why twenty? Each retailer gives us twelve jobs a month at market prices: installs at the brand's price, three-hundred-rupee visits, repairs at Urban Company's rates. That's nineteen and a half thousand. After technicians, parts, the retailer's commission and travel, five thousand and seventy is ours, and a one-lakh team divided by five thousand is twenty.
> **[click]** Add Protect, renewals and repairs on our own installs, and the same retailer reaches thirty thousand. A sixty-retailer cluster makes fifty-one lakh a year.

**Justify the 12 jobs.** Every price is net of GST and set at the market rate:

| Job | Count | Price | Market reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC install | 4 | ₹2,600 | ₹800 fee we keep (LG charter ₹1,199, less the service centre's share) + ₹1,800 bracket and 1 m of copper (LG: bracket ₹750, copper ₹1,050 a metre) |
| Other install | 2 | ₹300 | Market rate for a washing machine, fridge or purifier |
| Service visit | 1 | ₹300 | The same as the local mechanic, below Urban Company and LG's ₹500–900 |
| Repair with a part | 3 | ₹2,000 | About ₹2,360 with GST, averaged across Urban Company's rate card: capacitor ₹599–749, fan motor ₹2,299, gas ₹3,000, PCB ₹1,500–4,500 |
| AMC | 2 | ₹1,100 | ₹1,299 with GST. Urban Company charges ₹1,098–1,298 |
| **Total** | **12** | **₹19,500** | **₹1,625 is the average of the mix, not a price** |

**Justify the costs:**

| Cost | ₹ a month | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Technicians | 4,900 | About ₹408 a job. Step 6 pays ₹330–500 a job |
| Parts | 6,400 | 4 AC installs × about ₹1,200 of materials at cost, + 3 repairs × about ₹530 of parts |
| Retailer commission | 1,320 | 5% on installs (₹11,000 → ₹550) + 8% on visits and repairs (₹6,300 → ₹504) + 12% on AMC (₹2,200 → ₹264) |
| Travel, payments, AI desk, guarantee reserve | 1,810 | Fuel, payment fees, the AI desk (under ₹5 a job) and the 90-day guarantee reserve |
| **Contribution** | **5,070 (26%)** | ₹19,500 − ₹14,430 |
| **Breakeven** | **20 retailers** | ₹1,00,000 ÷ ₹5,070 = 19.7 |

**Justify the year-5 growth.** These are the same retailers; no new ones are needed:

| Added a month | ₹ | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 2 Protect plans | 3,400 | ₹1,700 each (₹2,000 with GST). That is about 4% of the roughly 55 covered appliances a retailer sells, below the pilot's 6% |
| 1 AMC renewal | 1,100 | Earlier customers renewing |
| 3 repairs on appliances we installed | 6,000 | ₹2,000 each, as the installed base ages |
| **Revenue** | **30,000** | 18 jobs a month (today's 12 + 2 plans + 1 renewal + 3 repairs). ₹19,500 + ₹10,500 = +54% from the same retailer; average ₹1,667 a job |

**Justify the cluster panel:**
- **Pilot, October:** 34 × ₹5,070 = ₹1.72 L, − the ₹1.0 L team = **+₹0.72 L**. This is the same figure as slide 6's chart.
- **Mature cluster:** 60 × ₹9,600 = ₹5.76 L, − a ₹1.5 L team = **+₹4.26 L a month = ₹51 L a year**.
  - The team adds a second field executive and a parts desk.
  - Revenue is 60 × ₹30,000 × 12 = ₹2.16 Cr, so ₹51 L is a 24% margin.
  - Breakeven for a mature cluster is 16 retailers.

**If they ask…**
- **"Why ₹1,625 a job when a washing-machine call is ₹300?"** Because ₹1,625 is an average. Three of our twelve jobs are ₹300 jobs. Four are AC installs that include materials, and three are repairs with a part.
- **"Are you cheaper than the mechanic?"** No, we charge the same. We win on speed, a guarantee and the record (slides 11–12).
- **"Year 1 shows ₹25,000. Why the jump from ₹19,500?"** Protect is sold from day one at every new retailer, and pilot customers start renewing their AMC.

## Slide 8: Families pay market prices per job. Keepwell keeps ₹26 of every ₹100. (Kavyanth, 0:20)

**On screen:**
- A bar splitting every ₹100: technician ₹25, parts ₹33, retailer ₹7, travel and payments ₹9, Keepwell ₹26.
- Three cards: Now (families pay), Year 2 (agencies and partners pay), Year 3 (brands and buyers pay).

**Say:**
> So the model is simple: families pay per job, at market prices. Of every hundred rupees, the technician gets twenty-five, parts thirty-three, the retailer seven, travel and payments nine, and we keep twenty-six. Later, agencies pay us for parts and brands pay to verify installs.

**Justify:**

| On the slide | Basis |
|---|---|
| ₹25 / ₹33 / ₹7 / ₹9 / ₹26 | Slide 7's month divided by ₹19,500: 4,900 → 25, 6,400 → 33, 1,320 → 7, 1,810 → 9, 5,070 → 26. They add to ₹100 |
| Now: install ₹2,600, visit ₹300, repair ₹2,000, AMC ₹1,299, Protect ₹2,000 | The slide 7 prices. AMC and Protect are quoted with GST, as the customer sees them |
| Year 2: genuine parts, partner clusters (10% fee), trade-ins | Parts are bought centrally and sold to local repair agencies. Existing service centres run new clusters under our brand and pay a 10% fee; Kavyanth knows 200+ service-centre owners |
| Year 3: verified installs and claims, refurbished units, e-waste (EPR) | Brands and plan sellers pay to verify installs and claims against our record, Sri Charan's background. Old units are refurbished and resold, or recycled under the E-Waste Rules 2022 |

**If they ask…**
- **"Is year 2–3 revenue in your plan?"** Only through the rise in revenue per retailer, from ₹19,500 to ₹30,000. Partner fees, verification and resale beyond that are upside the plan does not count (Step 7c).
- **"Why would a retailer accept 7%?"** The commission is on top. What the retailer really gets is relief from after-sale blame and a customer who keeps coming back (slide 4: retention).
