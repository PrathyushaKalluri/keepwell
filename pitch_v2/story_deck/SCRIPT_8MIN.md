# Keepwell seed pitch: the 8-minute story deck, explained slide by slide

**Deck:** [`index_8min.html`](index_8min.html). Open it in Chrome. **F** goes full screen, **N** shows these notes, **→ / space** reveals the next click and **B** opens the backup slides for Q&A.
**Length:** 12 slides, about **7:13** at 150 words a minute.
**Speaking time:** Prathyusha (CEO) 2:32 · Kavyanth (COO) 3:33 · Sri Charan (CTO) 1:08.

## How to use this script

Each slide has five parts:

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **SHOW** | What is on screen. It is the proof, so never read it aloud. |
| **SAY** | Word for word. `[click]` means press → once. |
| **WHY** | The job this slide does in the story. |
| **THEY SHOULD BELIEVE** | What the investor takes away when the slide ends. |
| **BRIDGE** | The last line, which carries the room into the next slide. |

**The screen and the voice do different jobs:** the screen proves and the voice explains. They repeat each other only once, on purpose, for the line we want remembered.

## Why the story runs in this order

1. **Feel it first (slides 1–2).** One shop owner's week, then the evidence and four numbers. The investor feels the problem before we name a product.
2. **Trust the people (slide 3).** The team comes straight after the problem, as the people who lived it, with one network each.
3. **The answer (slides 4–5).** Keepwell is named only now, as the answer to Ravi. One appliance's journey makes the product concrete.
4. **Proof, then economics (slides 6–7).** The pilot comes first so the numbers explain something real. One slide carries both cluster profit and the plan's margin.
5. **Why us, how big (slides 8–10).** The open lane, the market and the funded journey to exit.
6. **The ask, then the close (slides 11–12).** The requirement comes before the number, the gate protects the investor, and we return to Ravi with the problem solved.

### 1. Keepwell keeps the promise the retailer made

**SHOW (on screen):** Title: 'Keepwell keeps the promise the retailer made.' Tagline: the after-sale service arm of every local appliance retailer. Three founder cards. Vijayawada · Seed round · 2026.

**SAY (word for word):**

> Meet Ravi. He runs a multi-brand appliance store in Guntur. On Monday he sells a thirty-eight-thousand-rupee AC. By Thursday the family is back at his counter, because the installer hasn't come. Two years later it breaks, and a mechanic guesses.
>
> I'm Prathyusha, with Kavyanth and Sri Charan. We are Keepwell: the after-sale service arm of every local appliance retailer like Ravi.

**WHY this slide:** Open on one retailer's week while the title and the team are on screen: the investor knows the problem, the company and the people in 30 seconds.

**THEY SHOULD BELIEVE:** This is about the local retailer, and these three people know that world.

**BRIDGE:** *"Here is the problem in three parts."*

**SAY (word for word):**

And the retailer earns nothing after the sale, while courts hold him liable.

> [click] The result: the person the family trusts has no way to keep the promise he made.

**WHY this slide:** One problem, three causes, one result line, the same structure an investor scans in five seconds.

**THEY SHOULD BELIEVE:** The pain sits with the local retailer and the family, and nobody owns it.

### 5. The pilot cluster paid for itself from 20 retailers

**SHOW (on screen):** Left, one sum read top to bottom: 195 retailer-months × ₹19,500 = ₹38.0 L revenue (2,340 jobs) − ₹28.1 L job costs = ₹9.9 L contribution (26%) − ₹8.0 L cluster team = +₹1.9 L cluster profit. Right, the monthly chart with active retailers under each month: contribution crosses the ₹1.0 L team cost in May, at 20 retailers. [click] The result line.

**SAY (word for word):**

> We ran one cluster in Vijayawada–Guntur for eight months, from twelve retailers to thirty-four. Read the left side as one sum. Add up the active retailers each month and you get a hundred and ninety-five retailer-months. Each brought nineteen and a half thousand rupees, so thirty-eight lakh of revenue. After technicians, parts and the retailers' commission, nine point nine lakh was left. The cluster team cost eight lakh. So the cluster made one point nine lakh of profit.
>
> The chart shows when it turned: in May, the month we reached twenty retailers.
>
> [click] Each retailer leaves about five thousand rupees a month, so twenty retailers pay for the team. By October we were earning seventy-two thousand a month.

**WHY this slide:** Every number is a step in one sum, and the chart shows the month the sum turned positive. One point: the cluster pays for itself.

**THEY SHOULD BELIEVE:** The cluster model is profitable in the field, and they know exactly why.

### 6. Each retailer leaves ₹5,070 a month today and ₹9,600 at maturity
**Kavyanth (COO)** · about 0:56 · act: The proof

**SHOW (on screen):** Three panels, left to right. **SAY (word for word):**

> [click] Same unit, same arithmetic: fifty-one lakh a year per cluster.

**WHY this slide:** It opens up the ₹5,070 from slide 5, grows the same retailer, and ends on slide 5's October number before scaling it. A continuation, not a new set of facts.
