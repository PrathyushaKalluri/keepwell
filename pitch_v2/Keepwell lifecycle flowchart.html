Sale

Appliance age: day 0 · Keepwell: now

Where it happens

At the counter of an independent multi-brand appliance shop in a Tier 2/3 town. The shop adds the install and Protect to the bill, and the family agrees on WhatsApp.

Keepwell gets

A job lead, the customer's phone number and the product model. No money yet.

Everyone else gets

  • Customer: one number to call, and an install promised in 24 hours.
  • Shop: a service promise it does not have to run itself, and no blame if the brand installer is late.

Numbers

Install

Appliance age: day 1 · Keepwell: now

Where it happens

At the customer's home, within 24 hours. An authorised technician installs, captures the serial number and photos, and registers the warranty with the brand.

Keepwell gets

  • The install fee, and the materials it supplies.
  • An appliance record with a QR sticker on the unit.
  • The right to be called when it breaks.

Everyone else gets

  • Customer: install in a day, and the warranty stays valid because the installer is authorised.
  • Technician: paid per job within 48 hours.
  • Shop: a commission and no blame for delays.
  • Brand or service centre: its install target met in towns it cannot staff.

Numbers

  • An authorised AC install nets Keepwell ₹800Deck
  • About 4.0 installs per retailer a month, at about ₹2,100 each because AC installs include ₹1,500 of materials: ₹8,400 a monthGuess
  • Keepwell keeps about 24%, so about ₹500 an install and ₹2,016 a monthGuess
  • This line stays the same in year 1 and year 5Guess
QR sticker and record stay with the appliance

Trade-in, refurbish and resell

Appliance age: year 5 to 8 · Keepwell: pickups from year 2, resale from year 3

Where it happens

The exchange happens at the shop counter when the customer buys a new unit. Keepwell's field arm values and collects the old unit. Units that can be saved are refurbished and resold through retailers with a warranty.

Keepwell gets

  • A collection fee for the old unit.
  • The resale margin on units that can be sold again.

Everyone else gets

  • Customer: exchange credit towards a new unit.
  • Shop: a new sale (the loop back to stage 1) and a share of the resale.
  • Second-hand buyer: a working appliance with a warranty.
  • Technician: refurb jobs.

Numbers

Recycle

Appliance age: year 8 on · Keepwell: from year 3

Where it happens

The unit goes from the cluster hub to a registered e-waste recycler, with the paperwork the E-Waste Rules 2022 require. Working parts are pulled out first and go back into the parts stock.

Keepwell gets

  • A collection fee per kilogram.
  • Salvaged parts for stage 5.
  • Later, a share of the recycling certificates that brands must buy.

Everyone else gets

  • Brand: meets its legal take-back target (EPR).
  • Recycler: a steady supply of units with records.
  • Customer: safe disposal.

Numbers

  • Not in the deck's totals. The smallest line.
  • ₹10-15 a kilo for 30-60 kg a unit, so about ₹300-900 a unitGuess
  • About 55% of units end up hereGuess

In the cluster's training room for two weeks, then on the job with a mentor.

A certified, rated technician pool. Quality goes up and leakage goes down.

A parts store in each cluster, restocked centrally from distributors. Starts in year 2.

What slides 5 and 6 leave out

  1. No split of the ₹19,500. The deck never says how much is installs, repairs or AMC. I assumed 4.0 installs, 7.0 repairs and 1.3 AMC plans a month.
  2. The pilot has no Protect in it. The ₹19,500 comes without Protect, which was only a counter test. Yet about a quarter of the later ₹30,000 is Protect. Most of the growth rests on a product that has not been sold at scale or checked legally.
  3. The jump from 26% to 32% kept has no bridge. My bridge covers about ₹8,800 of the ₹9,600. The last ₹808 is a plug I cannot explain.
  4. The mature cluster cost is hidden. The pilot team costs ₹1.0 L a month. The ₹51 L a year only works if a mature cluster costs about ₹1.5 L a month, and the deck does not say so.
  5. The pilot profit is cluster-level only. The +₹1.9 L leaves out the head-office team: founders, engineers, AI and marketing.
  6. The pace of signing retailers jumps. The pilot added 22 retailers in 7 months, about 3 a month. Getting from 34 to 200 in 2027 needs about 14 a month, around four times fasterGuess.
  7. No cost of signing a retailer appears on slides 5 or 6, and there is no data on whether retailers stay past their first months, only that they signed 2-year agreements.

Reality check: two plans disagree about when the company makes money

At cluster level the deck's numbers are positive: a cluster breaks even at about 20 retailers. The question is the whole company, which also pays for head office and for opening new clusters.
