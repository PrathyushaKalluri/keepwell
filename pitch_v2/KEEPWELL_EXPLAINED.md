# Keepwell, explained from the beginning

*A plain-language guide to the whole pitch, written for someone who has never heard of Keepwell. It covers who the founders are, what problem they found, what they built, what happened when they tested it, how the company makes money, why it is raising money, and how every number connects to the next. Each section ends with the reasoning behind it.*

*All numbers come from the financial model (`keepwell_model.xlsx`) and the claims ledger (`claims_ledger.csv`). A short glossary is at the end.*

## 2. The problem they found

In small-town India, people buy appliances from a local retailer they know. It might be a single-counter dealer or a large multi-brand showroom. The pitch calls all of them **local retailers**: every independent appliance retailer in Tier 2 and 3 towns, of every size. The national chains are left out because they run their own service.

**Take Ravi, a retailer in Guntur.**
- On Monday he sells a ₹38,000 AC to a family he has known for years.
- The brand's installer says 2–5 days, so the family calls Ravi. On Thursday they are at his counter, not the brand's.
- Two years later the AC breaks. It is out of warranty. A local mechanic guesses at the fault, charges what he likes and keeps no record.

Ravi earns nothing after the sale, but he carries all the blame.

**This is common, not unusual.** The pitch backs it with sourced facts and its own discovery work:

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| A brand install takes **2–5 days** | Flipkart's terms (LG quotes 24–72 hours) |
| **80–91%** of machines in homes are out of warranty | Industry data |
| **44%** of AC service goes to local mechanics, with no fixed price, guarantee or record | LocalCircles survey, 13,487 people |
| A local retailer nets **2–3%** on a sale and earns nothing afterwards | Trade research |
| Consumer courts have still held sellers **jointly liable** with brands | Chennai ₹35,000; Hyderabad ₹60,000 |

**Discovery** ran from April to July 2025, in Vijayawada, Guntur, Warangal, Hyderabad and Visakhapatnam:
- **51 of 64** retailers said installation delays get blamed on them.
- **7 of 12** households kept no service record at all.
- The rest kept records on paper: an agency's sticker with the next date in marker, a handwritten repair diary, a refill date written on a gas cylinder.

**The reasoning.** The problem has a clear owner, the retailer, and a clear victim, the family. It sits between three parties who each serve only a slice:
- the brand handles the warranty;
- the mechanic handles the repair;
- marketplaces serve city households.

Nobody owns the appliance's life after the sale. The family's trust sits with the retailer, but the retailer has no way to keep the promise he made at the counter. That gap is the company.

## 3. What Keepwell is

**Keepwell is the after-sale service arm of every local appliance retailer.** Its line is: *the retailer sells the promise; Keepwell keeps it.*

The vision, in one sentence:

> *Keepwell looks after every appliance a local retailer sells for its whole life: installed right, repaired with genuine parts, kept covered, and refurbished or recycled when its time is up.*

**What the retailer can now sell, all under its own name:**
- **Authorised installation within 24 hours.** Keepwell installs under sub-contracts with brand-authorised service centres, so the warranty is not voided. Several brands void the warranty if anyone unauthorised installs.
- **Guaranteed repair and annual maintenance (AMC)** at a fixed rate card, for ACs, fridges, washing machines and water purifiers.
- **Keepwell Protect**, a 2-year repair plan (about ₹2,000) the retailer adds to the bill.
  - It is a **service contract that Keepwell fulfils itself**, not insurance.
  - Money for expected claims is kept separate.
  - The terms have been reviewed by a lawyer, and a formal legal opinion is due within six months of the funding.
- **The appliance record.** Every visit, part and plan is written to one record, with the family's consent, on WhatsApp.

**What Keepwell does, what it earns, and what everyone else gains, at each stage of an appliance's life:**

| | **1 Sold** (day 0) | **2 Installed** (day 1) | **3 Warranty years** (years 0–2) | **4 Covered** (AMC + Protect) | **5 Repaired** (years 2–8) | **6 Traded in** (years 5–8 · refurbish) | **7 Recycled** (year 8+) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Keepwell does** | Opens the appliance record on WhatsApp; family consents | Authorised install in 24 h; serial, photos, warranty registered | Keeps the record and sends reminders (the brand repairs) | Sells AMC and Protect; off-season service visits | AI desk drafts fault and part; genuine part; photo + OTP close | Collects the old unit; refurbishes; resells through retailers | Sends to a registered recycler; salvages parts |
| **Keepwell gets** | The lead and the customer | ₹2,600 per AC install | No fee: the relationship that sells the plan | AMC ₹1,299 · Protect ₹2,000 | ₹300 visit · ₹2,000 repair · parts margin | Pickup fee + resale margin (upside) | Per-kg fee + salvaged parts (upside) |
| **Family** | One number to call; install promised in 24 h | Installed in a day; warranty stays valid | Free brand repairs; nothing forgotten | Scheduled service; repairs covered after warranty | Price fixed upfront; 90-day guarantee | Exchange credit for the old unit | Safe, legal disposal |
| **Retailer** | Sells the promise, not just the box | 5% commission; no blame for delays | Stays in touch through reminders | 12% on AMC · 25% on Protect | 8% commission; keeps the customer | The next sale: back to stage 1 | Old units off the shop floor |
| **Technician** | — | Paid per job within 48 h | — | Off-season visits fill the quiet months | Right part on the first visit; paid in 48 h | Refurbishing work | — |
| **Brands and partners** | — | Service centre: installs done in towns it can't staff | Brand: a verified install and serial | — | Parts distributor: one pooled order | Second-hand buyer: a working unit with a warranty | Brand: meets its e-waste take-back target |

Stage 5 → 6: when an appliance isn't worth repairing, it becomes a trade-in, and the trade-in is the retailer's next sale (back to stage 1). Stages 1–5 are live now; trade-in, refurbishing and recycling start in year 3 and are upside, not in the plan's numbers. Every stage is written to one consented appliance record.

**Where the AI fits.** It reads voice notes and photos, and drafts the job. Prices, safety stops and claim decisions are always made by rules and people. The AI keeps costs low; the advantage over competitors comes from the retailers, the density of clusters and the record.

**The order of building:**
1. **Serve:** installs, repairs, AMC and Protect. This is live now.
2. **Supply:** central parts buying, partner clusters run by existing service centres, and trade-ins. This comes next.
3. **Know and circulate:** the record lets brands verify installs and claims, and old units get a second life. This comes later.

**The reasoning.**
- **Why work under the retailer's name:** families trust a person, not a system. Keepwell borrows the retailer's trust instead of competing for the customer, so it doesn't have to buy customers through ads.
- **Why install first:** installation happens at the moment of sale and is where the record starts. Every later service depends on that first visit.
- **Why four appliances, not just ACs:** AC work peaks in summer, so fridges, washing machines, purifiers and AMC visits keep technicians busy the rest of the year.
- **Why the record matters:** each service makes the next one easier to sell, and the record eventually becomes valuable to brands.

## 5. The test: one cluster, eight months

A **cluster** is a group of local retailers in nearby towns, served by one local team. The team is a coordinator, a dispatcher and a field executive, plus partner technicians who are paid per job.

Keepwell ran one cluster in the Vijayawada–Guntur belt from **March to October 2026**. It started with 12 retailers and grew to 34.

**All the pilot numbers are 8-month totals for this one cluster, and each one follows from the one before:**

| Number | What it is | How it links |
|---|---|---|
| **34 retailers** | All on 2-year exclusive service agreements (33 still active in November) | Grew 12 → 15 → 20 → 24 → 28 → 30 → 32 → 34, month by month |
| **₹19,500** | Revenue per active retailer per month | 12 jobs a month × ₹1,625 per job |
| **₹38 lakh** | Total revenue | Active retailers each month × ₹19,500, added up over 8 months |

| **+₹1.9 lakh** | Cluster profit | ₹9.9 L − ₹8 L |
| **6%** | Share of buyers who took Keepwell Protect | Tested at 12 retailers in the last 8 weeks |

**Month by month (₹ lakh):**

| | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**The one point the pilot proves: a cluster pays for itself.** Each retailer adds about ₹5,070 a month of contribution. The team costs ₹1 lakh a month, so **about 20 retailers cover it**. **The operations also worked:**

- 33 of 34 retailers stayed.

**The reasoning.** The pilot was designed to answer one question, and every number on the traction slide answers it from a different angle. The monthly table shows *when*: at 20 retailers. The breakeven arithmetic shows *why*: ₹1 lakh ÷ ₹5,070 per retailer. That is why the deck shows them together.

## 6. How Keepwell makes money

**Who pays today.** Families pay, through the retailer, for:
- an authorised AC install: a **₹800** fee (after the service centre's share of the brand's rate) plus about **₹1,800** of bracket and copper pipe;
- a service visit: **from ₹300**; a repair with a part: about **₹2,000** before GST (₹2,360 with it), averaged across part types;
- AMC: **₹1,299** a year;
- **Keepwell Protect**: about **₹2,000** for two years.

**The retailer earns a commission on every job.** That, together with being freed from the after-sale blame, is why retailers hand over the work.

**Where ₹1,625 a job comes from.** Most jobs are small: a washing-machine install or a service visit is about ₹300. The average is higher because a third of the jobs are AC installs, which include the bracket and extra copper pipe, and three a month are repairs that need a part. One retailer's month in the pilot:

| The 12 jobs behind ₹19,500 (one retailer, a month) | Jobs | ₹ each, net of GST | ₹ a month | Why this price (market reference) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AC installation, authorised | 4 | 2,600 | 10,400 | The brand's own price. LG's charter: ₹1,199 fee (Keepwell keeps ₹800, the service centre the rest), bracket ₹750, copper ₹1,050 a metre. Same price, done in 24 hours instead of 2–5 days |
| Other installation: washing machine, fridge, purifier | 2 | 300 | 600 | The market rate. Low margin, but it starts the appliance record |
| Service visit, labour only (e.g. a washing-machine check) | 1 | 300 | 300 | Matches the local mechanic, below the ₹500–900 basic service of Urban Company and LG. Price is never the reason to leave |
| Repair with a part: capacitor, fan motor, gas top-up, PCB | 3 | 2,000 | 6,000 | About ₹2,360 with GST, averaged across part types on Urban Company's rate card (capacitor ₹599–749, fan motor ₹2,299, gas ₹3,000, PCB ₹1,500–4,500). Genuine part and a 90-day guarantee |
| AMC, 2 AC services a year | 2 | 1,100 | 2,200 | ₹1,299 with GST, the top of Urban Company's ₹1,098–1,298; adds the record and reminders |
| **Total** | **12** | **average 1,625** | **19,500** | **₹1,625 is the average of the mix, not a price.** A third of the jobs are AC installs with materials, which lifts the average above the ₹300 jobs |

**Protect is not in the pilot month.** It was sold only in the pilot's last 8 weeks (72 plans) and is booked over its 2-year term, so it enters from year 1. Protect sells at ₹2,000 with GST (₹1,700 net) for 2 years; Croma ZipCare charges ₹1,139–2,499 for 1 year.

**The pricing rule:** Keepwell charges the market rate on every job and wins on speed, the guarantee and the record, not on price.

**Where a retailer's ₹19,500 a month goes (pilot):**

| | ₹ a month | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue: 12 jobs × ₹1,625 | 19,500 | The jobs a retailer passes to Keepwell |
| − Technicians | 4,900 | Partner technicians are paid per job, ₹330–500 (about ₹400 on average) |
| − Parts | 6,400 | Genuine parts used in repairs |
| − Retailer commission | 1,320 | 5% on installs, 8% on repairs, 12% on AMC, 25% on Protect |
| − Travel, payments, AI desk, guarantee reserve | 1,810 | Small costs that come with every job |
| **= Contribution (26%)** | **5,070** | What Keepwell keeps from one retailer, before the cluster team |

**Why 20 retailers is the breakeven:** the pilot cluster's team costs ₹1 lakh a month, and ₹1,00,000 ÷ ₹5,070 ≈ 20. That is exactly when the pilot turned profitable (section 5).

**How the same retailer grows from ₹19,500 to ₹30,000 a month by year 5:**

| | ₹ a month | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot revenue | 19,500 | |
| + Protect plans | 3,400 | 2 plans a month × ₹1,700 net of GST: about 4% of the ~55 covered appliances a retailer sells each month. The pilot's counter test reached 6% |
| + AMC renewals | 1,100 | 1 renewal a month × ₹1,100 net of GST, as earlier customers come up for renewal |
| + Repairs on appliances Keepwell installed | 6,000 | 3 more repairs a month × ₹2,000 as the installed base grows |
| **= Revenue** | **30,000** | |
| **Contribution at 32%** | **9,600** | Higher than 26% because parts are bought centrally and denser routes mean more jobs per technician day |

No new retailers are needed for this growth; it comes from the same ones. Year 1 of the plan sits on the way, at ₹25,000 a month.

**From one retailer to one cluster (a month):**

| | Pilot cluster, October | Mature cluster |
|---|---|---|
| Retailers × contribution | 34 × ₹5,070 = ₹1.72 L | 60 × ₹9,600 = ₹5.76 L |
| − Team | ₹1.0 L | ₹1.5 L (adds a second field executive and a parts desk) |
| **= Cluster profit** | **+₹0.72 L** | **+₹4.26 L a month = ₹51 L a year (24% of ₹2.16 Cr revenue)** |

The October figure is the same ₹0.72 L the pilot chart ends on. The mature cluster is the same arithmetic with more retailers, each worth more.

**Later income** (upside, not counted in the plan beyond that rise):
- parts sold to local repair agencies;
- partner clusters run by existing service centres, for a 10% fee;
- trade-in pickups;
- brands paying to verify installs and claims;
- refurbished resale and e-waste.

**The reasoning.** The whole business rests on one unit: the active retailer. If you know how many retailers Keepwell serves and how much each brings in, you know the revenue. If you know how many clusters it runs, you know the main cost. That makes the business easy to check, which is why the pitch shows one chain (job → retailer → cluster) and not twenty revenue lines.

**How each line is built:**
- **Revenue** = average retailers in the year × revenue per retailer.
- **Contribution** = revenue × the margin.
- **EBITDA** = contribution − cluster teams − head office.

**Why it is profitable from year one:**
- **The pilot cluster is already profitable.**
- **New clusters fill fast,** because the founders' networks bring retailers in cheaply. The field executive who signs them up is already counted inside the cluster team's cost.
- **Head office starts lean:** the founders, two engineers and a finance lead, with the AI desk keeping dispatch staff low.

A full cluster team costs about ₹1.5 lakh a month, so at about ₹7,000 of contribution per retailer, a full cluster breaks even at about 22 retailers.

**If things go slower (the cautious case):**
- Revenue per retailer is 10% lower, margins are 2 points lower, clusters open 25% more slowly and head office is 15% smaller.
- Year one breaks even (−₹0.05 Cr), and year two onwards is profitable.

**The reasoning.** A profitable services business still needs cash to grow, which the next section explains. Being profitable from year one means investor money is not used to cover losses. That is rare at seed stage and lowers the risk.

## 8. What they are asking for, and why exactly that much

**The ask: ₹2.5 crore in a seed round.** It is raised in a single close as **CCPS** (compulsorily convertible preference shares, the standard instrument for Indian priced rounds), at a **₹10 crore pre-money valuation**. Investors get **20%** of the company (₹2.5 Cr of a ₹12.5 Cr post-money company).

**Why raise at all, if the business is profitable?**
- **Opening a cluster costs about ₹20 lakh before it earns:**
  - set-up: ₹5 L;
  - a stock of parts: ₹8 L;
  - signing up retailers and training technicians: ₹7 L.
- **Working capital grows with revenue:** parts on the shelf, and payments from brands for installs that arrive after the work is done.

Profits alone would open clusters slowly. The seed money lets Keepwell open eight new clusters in two years.

| Buffer and compliance | 20% | ₹0.5 Cr | The Protect legal opinion, audits, and a cushion above the ₹2.0 Cr peak |

**What investors can check along the way:**
- **By month 9 (December 2027), a board checkpoint:**
  - three clusters live and a fourth opening;
  - each cluster past its breakeven;
  - the Protect legal opinion in hand.
- **By month 18 (September 2028):**
  - six or more clusters and 350+ retailers;
  - a revenue run-rate of ₹8 Cr or more;
  - profit every quarter.

**Who they want to raise from:**

| Investor | Amount | Why them |
|---|---|---|
| A lead seed fund or angel network | ₹1.25 Cr | Sets the price and takes a board seat |
| Strategic angels: former brand service heads, a dealers' association leader, a field-service founder | ₹0.75 Cr | They open doors to brands, partners and retailers outside the two states |

**The reasoning.**
- **Why ₹2.5 Cr:** the ask is not a round number picked for comfort. It is the plan's peak cash need (₹2.0 Cr) plus a buffer.
- **Why one round:** it covers everything up to the point where the company pays for itself, so investors aren't relying on a later round to survive.
- **The use of funds matches the model:** launches and working capital, net of profits, add up to that ₹2.0 Cr.
- **Course method check:** the course says to size a round on at least a year of need. Keepwell's first-year need is about ₹0.8 Cr. It chooses to raise enough for the whole rollout to 1,000 retailers in one go, rather than raise again in 2028.

## 9. Why ₹10 crore, and what an investor gets back

**Three ways of pricing a seed company all support ₹10 crore pre-money:**

| Method | Result | How it is worked out |
|---|---|---|
| **The course's scorecard method** | ₹10.0 Cr | A ₹9 Cr benchmark × Keepwell's score of 89/100 ÷ the benchmark score of 80 |
| **Comparable Indian seed rounds** | ₹6–12 Cr | 2025–26 rounds of a similar size |
| **The VC method** | Up to ₹47 Cr post-money | Price the exit, then work back at a 40% yearly return over 5 years. Keepwell's ₹12.5 Cr post-money is far below this ceiling |

**Other ways to sense-check it:**
- ₹10 Cr is about **2.9 times year-one revenue**, for a company that is profitable in year one.
- It is about **twice the post-money cap** the pre-seed angels got, which is fair for a company that has since proven its pilot.

**The score of 89/100** comes from the course's fundability matrix:

| Area | Score | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| Market | 13/15 | Large and growing |
| Product | 26/30 | Profitable at every level; the whole appliance life on one record |
| Startup | 41/45 | Founders with the exact networks needed; clean company records |
| X-factor | 9/10 | Regional roots, CIE-IIITH, timing, and a one-line story |

**Who owns what after the seed:**

| Holder | Share |
|---|---|
| Founders | 64.0% (Prathyusha 24.3%, Kavyanth 19.8%, Sri Charan 19.8%) |
| Pre-seed angels (their iSAFE converts at the ₹6 Cr cap) | 6.0% |
| Employee option pool | 10.0% |
| **Seed investors** | **20.0%** |

**Terms.** The structure follows the course's model term sheet:
- **Preference and protection:**
  - **1x non-participating preference:** if the company is sold cheaply, investors get their money back first.
  - **Weighted-average anti-dilution:** limits how much investors are diluted if a later round is priced lower.
- **Board and oversight:**
  - a three-person board: two founders and the lead investor;
  - investor approval for budgets, debt and any change to how Protect is structured;
  - monthly reporting.
- **Founder commitments:** founders cannot sell for three years, and their shares vest over four years.

**The return.**
- **The exit:** in year 5 (March 2032), Keepwell would have 50 clusters, 3,000 retailers, ₹86 Cr of revenue and ₹16.9 Cr of EBITDA.
- **The value:** a buyer paying 15 times EBITDA values the company at **₹254 crore**, about 2.9 times that year's revenue.
- **The seed's share:** the seed investors' 20% would be worth about **₹51 crore, roughly 20 times their money, or an 83% return a year** (before any optional later round).
- **Likely buyers:**
  - warranty and after-sales platforms (Onsitego bought Qdigi; Asurion bought uBreakiFix);
  - appliance brands and retail chains;
  - insurers;
  - private-equity firms building home-services groups.

**The reasoning.** The price is set where the course's own method lands, and it stays inside what the market pays. That leaves a large gap below the VC-method ceiling, so the investor is not paying for perfection. The return comes from a profitable operator growing into a ₹250 crore company in five years, not from a software-style valuation.

## 10. The market, the competition, and why now

**The competition: market prices, more for the money.**

| Player | What it charges | What you get | What's missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Keepwell** | Brand's install price (₹1,199 + materials) · ₹300 visit · AMC ₹1,299 · Protect ₹2,000 for 2 years | Authorised install in 24 h, fixed price, 90-day guarantee, one record, under the retailer's name | — |
| **Brand service centre** | ₹1,199 + GST for a split-AC install (LG, Voltas) | Authorised; warranty kept | 2–5 days; one brand only; thin in small towns |
| **Urban Company** | AC install from ₹799 · gas ₹3,000 · AMC ₹1,098–1,298 | App booking, trained pros | 51 cities, 90%+ of value in the top 10; not the retailer's |
| **Onsitego · Croma ZipCare** | Plans at chain counters: ZipCare ₹1,139–2,499 a year | A plan sold with the appliance | No installs; no own repair crew in small towns |
| **Jeeves · resQ** | resQ window-AC install ₹531–590 | Install and repair | Only Flipkart or Reliance buyers; fast install in 13 cities |
| **Local mechanic** | ₹300 a visit | Nearby and cheap | No guarantee, no record; his AC installs void the warranty |

**Why it's hard to copy, or to enter:**
1. **Exclusive retailer agreements:** 2-year exclusive service agreements; 33 of 34 pilot retailers stayed.
2. **Authorised installs:** Sub-contracts with brand service centres; without them an install voids the warranty.
3. **Density costs time and money:** A cluster loses money until 20 retailers; warm introductions sign one for ₹9.5k, cold ₹22k.
4. **The record compounds:** Every job adds to one consented appliance record: the next lead, verified claims.
5. **Incumbents are built for someone else:** UC: metro homes · Onsitego: plans, no crew · Jeeves, resQ: own buyers · brands: one brand.

**Easier to buy than to build: Onsitego paid ₹80 Cr for Qdigi (2024). That is our exit, not our threat.** A big player that wants this market would find it faster to buy a built network of retailers and clusters than to build one, which is exactly what Onsitego did with Qdigi.

**Why now:**

| | Why now |
|---|---|
| **~10%** | of Indian homes own an AC; the AC market grows 15% a year, ₹53,600 Cr (2025) → ₹93,610 Cr (2029) |
| **65%** | of Blue Star's sales are in Tier III–V towns; 85% of Voltas buyers are first-time buyers |
| **80–91%** | of appliances in homes are out of warranty: the paying after-sale years are here |
| **28% → 18%** | GST on ACs cut in September 2025: cheaper ACs, more first-time buyers |
| **< ₹5** | a job for AI in Telugu voice and WhatsApp: a small-town service desk is now affordable |
| **2022–25** | E-Waste Rules (EPR), the Right to Repair portal, DPDP consent and the labour codes reward a registered operator that keeps records |

**The market.**

**What the market is:** about **₹4,000 Cr a year**, spent installing, servicing and repairing the ACs, fridges, washing machines and water purifiers that families buy from local retailers in Tier 2/3 India.

| How it is built | |
|---|---|
| Local appliance retailers | 45,000 |
| × after-sale spend a year by each retailer's customers | ≈ ₹9 L |
| **= Total (TAM)** | **≈ ₹4,000 Cr** (range ₹3,500–4,800 Cr; household cross-check ₹3,600 Cr) |

| What it is spent on | Share | ₹ a year |
|---|---|---|
| Repair and service | 71% | ≈ ₹2,840 Cr |
| Installs | 26% | ≈ ₹1,040 Cr |
| AMC | 3% | ≈ ₹120 Cr |

**Who takes it today:** brand centres while the warranty runs, local mechanics after (44% of AC service), with no fixed price, guarantee or record. Nobody runs it for the retailer.

**The opportunity, layer by layer:**

| Layer | Size |
|---|---|
| India, Tier 2/3 (45,000 retailers) | ≈ ₹4,000 Cr |
| Five southern states, where we go by year 5 | ≈ ₹1,040 Cr |
| Andhra Pradesh + Telangana, where we start | ≈ ₹360 Cr |
| **Keepwell in year 5 (3,000 retailers)** | **₹86 Cr: about 2%** |

**The one point:** we need 3,000 of 45,000 retailers and about 2% of the spend to build a ₹86 Cr company.

**The reasoning.** The gap exists because each player started from a different customer: the household, the brand, or the chain store. Keepwell starts from the local retailer, who already holds the family's trust. That is hard to copy for three reasons:
- the retailer agreements are exclusive;
- the clusters are dense;
- the founders' networks make signing up the next retailer cheaper than it would be for a newcomer.

## 11. What could go wrong, and what is in place for it

| Risk | What protects against it |
|---|---|
| Growth is slower than planned | In the cautious case the business breaks even in year one and is profitable from year two. Peak cash rises to ₹2.6 Cr, covered by the ₹0.5 Cr buffer plus a three-month delay in two cluster launches |
| Protect is treated as insurance by regulators | It is a repairs-only service contract on lawyer-reviewed terms, with claims money kept separate. The formal legal opinion is due by month 6. If needed, it can be sold through a licensed insurer instead |
| Brands refuse to authorise installs | The two sub-contracts are renewed for 2027, and one brand has signed a letter of intent for direct authorisation |
| Retailers leave | Their name is on every job, they earn a commission, and they shed the blame. 33 of 34 pilot retailers stayed |
| Technicians leave | Paid per job within 48 hours, more jobs a day from cluster density, and accident cover. 1 of 22 left in the pilot |
| The founders lack exit experience | An advisor who scaled home services, and the investor's director on the board |

**The month-9 board checkpoint is the safety valve.** If the numbers fall short, expansion stops before more money is spent.

## 12. How it all links together, in one chain

1. **The founders' networks** give Keepwell retailers, service centres and a product from day one.
2. **The problem** sits with the local retailer, who holds the family's trust but cannot keep the after-sale promise.
3. **Keepwell** keeps that promise for the appliance's whole life, under the retailer's name.
4. **The pilot** proved a cluster pays for itself at 20 retailers. It made ₹1.9 L on ₹38 L of revenue.
5. **Each retailer** brings ₹19,500 a month today, rising to ₹30,000 as Protect, AMC and parts build.
6. **More retailers and more clusters** give ₹3.5 Cr of revenue in year one and ₹86 Cr in year five, **profitable every year**.
7. **Opening clusters** needs cash before they earn. The plan's peak need is ₹2.0 Cr, so Keepwell asks for ₹2.5 Cr.
8. **₹10 Cr pre-money** is where the course scorecard, comparable rounds and the VC method agree.
9. **A year-5 exit** at 15 times EBITDA is worth ₹254 Cr, about 20 times the seed investors' money.

**Closing line:** *Keepwell keeps the promise the retailer made when it sold the appliance.*

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Local retailer** | Any independent appliance retailer in Tier 2/3 towns, from a small dealer to a large showroom (not national chains) |
| **Cluster** | A group of nearby retailers served by one local team (coordinator, dispatcher, field executive) and partner technicians |
| **Revenue** | What customers pay Keepwell, after GST |
| **Contribution** | Revenue minus the direct cost of each job: technician pay, parts, the retailer's commission, small per-job costs |
| **EBITDA** | Profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Here: contribution minus cluster teams minus head office |
| **Breakeven** | The number of retailers whose contribution exactly pays for a cluster's team (20 in the pilot) |
| **Working capital** | Cash tied up in stock and in payments not yet received |
| **AMC** | Annual maintenance contract: paid yearly servicing |
| **Keepwell Protect** | A 2-year repair plan sold on the retailer's bill, delivered by Keepwell itself |
| **Pre-money / post-money** | The company's value before / after the new investment (₹10 Cr / ₹12.5 Cr) |
| **CCPS** | Compulsorily convertible preference shares: the standard instrument for an Indian priced round |
| **iSAFE** | An agreement for future shares, used for the pre-seed. It converts at the next round at a capped price |
| **Valuation cap** | The highest valuation at which an iSAFE converts (₹6 Cr post-money here) |
| **1x non-participating preference** | On a sale, investors get either their money back first or their share of the proceeds, whichever is higher |
| **IRR** | The yearly rate of return an investment earns |
| **TAM / SAM / beachhead** | The whole market / the part Keepwell can reach / the first region it starts in |
