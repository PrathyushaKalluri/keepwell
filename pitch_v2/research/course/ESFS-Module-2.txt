Concept of Dilution

  When Company Issues New Shares, Total Share Capital Goes Up
  Hence, Percentage of Ownership of Existing Shareholders reduces (diluted)
  Example:
     Company has 800,000 shares fully owned by founders
     Issues 200,000 shares to Angel Investor
     Your Holding of the Company comes down from 100% to ?%


     Dilution —> Effect of Issue of New Shares on Existing Shareholders Holding Percentage

Concept of Debt Servicing

  When Company takes Loans / Debt, there is a need to service them
     Servicing is through repayment of interest and repayment of loan amount
  In this, ownership of the shareholders does not get diluted (non dilutive)




     Debt Servicing —> Need for repayment of loan and payment of interest

Concept of Shares

  A Share is an unit of ownership of the Company
  Shareholders have various powers in the running of the Company
     Appoints the board,
     Approves the total loan that can be raised
     Approves the Financials, Auditors and other key affairs of the Company
  There are various types of Shares

Types of Shares




 Equity Shares                   Preference Shares




 Convertible Preference Shares   Options (ESOP, Warrants etc.)

                                                                 10
Equity Shares

  Simplest form of Ownership, also called Ordinary shares
  Has Voting Rights in proportion to the shares held on total shares of the Company
  On Liquidation, eligible for residual claim only after debts and any preferences
  Typically founders, management and angel rounds happens in Equity

                                                                                      11
Preference Shares

  Will have Preferential Rights over Equity
     Eg. Priority payout in liquidation after debt, before Equity
     First right for dividends before Equity holders
  Usually no voting rights
     Cannot influence key decisions
  Can have clauses pertaining to downside protection
     Risk mitigation for investors

                                                                    12
Convertible Preference Shares

  Kind of Preference Shares which can be converted to equity shares
  Automatic conversion on events or time or both
  Most preferred by VC / PE investors in a startup
     Separate for Each Series (Series A Preference Shares, Series B Preference Shares etc.)
     Increasing complexity on dilution, anti-dilution, preferences with each series
  Generally seek voting rights similar to Equity

                                                                                              13
Options

  Employees Stock Option Plans (ESOP)
     Reserved for Employees (and sometimes Advisors)
     Options convertible to Equity after vesting
     Vesting, typically happens over a period of 3 to 5 years based on the scheme
     Used to attract good talent, with low cash outflow (salaries) buyt high upside (equity)
  Warrants / Right to Subscribe
     Issued to Founders typically to incentivise retention and performance

Debt Instruments

                   15
Debt: When and Why for Startups

  It is a loan and has repayment and interest payment obligations
  Cash flow pressure increases with servicing of debt and interest
     Viable when there is a predictable revenue stream
  No dilution, control retained
     Reduces equity raising needs and thus dilution

                                                                     16
Bank/NBFC Loans

 Bank/NBFCs offer Loans
    Have fixed interest and repayment schedules
 Collateral/security requirements common
    Assets of the company, founders shares
 Personal Guarantee of founder insisted
 Suitable only when there is a predictable revenue and cashflow

                                                                  17
Non-Convertible Debentures

  Tradable debt security instrument
     Fixed interest, maturity term
  Debt market exists, like for equity
     Debentures can be listed and traded
  Cannot convert to equity
  Ideal for later-stage, revenue generating capex driven startups
  Credit rating needs / restriction on individuals to invest

                                                                    18
Venture Debt

  Non-dilutive, typically post-Series A
  Warrants/options combined with debt for equity upside
  Ideal for lower cost stable businesses or capex heavy businesses
  VC funds specialised offering
     InnoVen, Trifecta, Alteria

                                                                     19
Convertible Instruments

                          20
Convertibles: Debt-to-Equity

  Start as debt, converts to equity later
  Typically used during Bridge rounds, uncertain valuations
     Defers valuation/pricing until milestone
  Flexible for both founders and investors during uncertain times
  Popular in the early-stage Indian market

                                                                    21
Type of Convertible Debentures

  Convertible Debentures, Becomes Equity on Conversion
     Compulsory or Optional
  Indian VC standard practice
     Trigger events: funding, time

                                                         22
SAFE: Simple Agreement for Future Equity

  Unique convertible
     there is no interest dues or a maturity date
  Converts to equity on next equity financing event
  Conversion price set by valuation cap or at a discount
     to valuation arrived at during financing event
  Investor only gets equity when company raises priced round
  Founder-friendly: minimal legal complexity, popular in the US, iSAFE getting started in India

                                                                                                  23
Convertibles vs Equity for Founders

  Positives
     Flexibility postpone valuation decision to later date
     Founder-friendly transition rounds
  Risk
     Cap table complexity
     Requires very clear terms and understading

Typical Investor Rights in Funding Rounds

  Valuation is done on a Fully Diluted Basis
     Assuming all convertibles are fully converted.
  Founder and Employee Equity Vesting process defined
     For enhanced investor confidence.
     Typically includes a one-year cliff, with gradual release (for both Options and Equities sometimes).
     ESOP: Grant   → Vesting → Exercise
  Board Seats and Voting Rights are defined.
  Other Key Terms:
     Exit/buyback terms are crucial, especially in terms of an early exit.
     Anti-dilution rights affect future prices, specifically in case of down-valued rounds.
     Tag Along / Drag Along Rights.

Funding Myths & Practicalities
                                 46
Myth 1 – Every Startup Must Raise Capital

      External funding is not always required.


      Bootstrapping and organic revenue growth are often overlooked.


      Raising capital prematurely can lead to unnecessary equity dilution.


      Many businesses thrive with self-sustainable models.


      Align funding strategies with actual business needs.
                                                                             47
Myth 2 – Failure to Raise Means Failure for the Business

      Fundraising issues not always fatal


      Many survive on revenues alone


      Pivot or cost-management can rescue


      Alternative funding sources exist


      Resilience trumps constant capital chase
                                                           48
Myth 3 – Debt Is Always Risky

      Debt preserves equity ownership


      Suitable when cash flows are predictable
                                                     49

      Interest cost vs dilution trade-off


      Structured properly, debt enables growth


      Avoid over-leverage through careful planning
Myth 4 – Grants Are Free Money

      Grants demand strict compliance


      Milestones and reporting can be burdensome


      Limited amounts, not long-term funding


      Builds credibility, but doesn't scale


      Best for R&D and proof-of-concept phases
Myth 5 – Convertible Notes Always Founder-Friendly

      Conversion terms may dilute heavily


      Valuation caps and discounts matter


      Complex triggers can disadvantage founders


      Read term sheets for hidden clauses


      Not a default "easy" option
                                                     51
Myth 6 – Every Investment Is Good

      More capital isn't always beneficial


      Wrong investor can derail strategy


      Beware over-valuation and harsh terms


      Fit investor to stage and vision


      Quality of capital matters most

Term Sheet Essentials for Founders

      Liquidation preference dictates payout order


      Anti-dilution clauses protect investor shares


      Board composition affects governance control


      Vesting schedules align long-term incentives


      Read and negotiate every term
